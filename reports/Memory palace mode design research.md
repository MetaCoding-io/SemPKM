# Walk the Graph: Designing SemPKM's Memory Palace

SemPKM should build its memory palace as a **2D, user-authored, spatially stable map of small, visually distinct rooms**, each bound to a named graph and a handful of typed objects, with a deliberate "pin" action per placement, a visible route for anything ordered, and a three-tier display model in which sprites carry coarse state, a pixel-framed htmx overlay carries reading, and the existing GridStack dashboard carries editing. The controlled evidence says a software palace beats no strategy by roughly 10 to 28 percentage points on list-like recall and that a briefly explored virtual environment works as well as a lifelong-familiar one, but it also says the gain comes from active binding and distinct places, not from 3D or VR: head-tracked VR added only 8.8 points over a mouse-driven desktop in the best-known study, 3D added nothing measurable in classic spatial-UI trials, and roughly one in six VR participants drop out from cybersickness. The strongest design levers are therefore cheap ones SemPKM already controls: self-generated layouts (d ≈ 0.43 over assigned), dissimilar rooms (d = 0.91 vs −0.41 for similar loci), an explicit click-to-place act (+28%), low per-room density, no auto-rearrangement, and tight integration with search and the typed graph so the palace is an additional retrieval path rather than a silo. Arriving at a place should follow the "approach, prompt, panel" grammar that WorkAdventure, Gather and hobbyist RPG portfolios converged on independently; the Study opens a compact research view in a non-pausing overlay, with one more keypress dropping into the real dashboard. Sharing should start with Animal Crossing's dream-island model (publish a revocable snapshot of the rooms bound to a `urn:sempkm:shared:*` graph, visitors leave template-constrained traces as RDF, nothing needs a live host), because presence-only live visiting is a few hundred lines of FastAPI WebSocket code that can be layered on later, while audio and video are what killed the pandemic-era virtual-office category. Technically, a hand-rolled Canvas 2D renderer or melonJS (MIT, ~250 KB minzipped, native Tiled support) fits the esbuild vanilla-JS frontend, a strict subset of Tiled's TMJ format should hold the tiles while rooms and placements live in RDF, CC0 16-pixel art (Kenney, Ninja Adventure) avoids the licence traps of LPC and Limezu, and a parallel DOM "room list" plus an ARIA live region is the only credible accessibility story for a canvas world. The honest framing is that this mode is an engagement, organisation and re-encounter feature with a plausible recall bonus, not a proven memory multiplier: the best meta-analytic estimate for young adults is d ≈ 0.42 with very strong publication bias, no controlled study of a 2D top-down palace exists, and the only eight-week in-the-wild study saw users abandon a palace that lacked text entry and sat apart from their daily tools.

## The evidence supports 2D, user-built, stable rooms, not immersion

The replicated finding across two decades of memory-palace software is "any explorable environment with active placement beats no strategy", not "more immersion beats less". Legge, Madan, Ng and Caplan gave **142 participants** five minutes in a desktop apartment, school or warehouse and found both method-of-loci groups recalled **10 to 16 percent more** than uninstructed controls, with the virtual environment statistically equivalent to participants' own homes and the specific layout making no difference ([BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/memory-palace-mnemonic-strategy-works-virtual-environments)). Fassbender and Heiden's desktop 3D castle, the earliest "place icons where you like" system, showed a **25 percent recall advantage over a word list after one week**, as summarised in Krokos et al.'s review ([Krokos et al. PDF](https://obj.umiacs.umd.edu/virtual_reality_study/10.1007-s10055-018-0346-3.pdf)). The study most often cited for VR, Krokos, Plaisant and Varshney, is in fact modest: **84.05 percent recall in a head-mounted display versus 75.24 percent on a 30-inch desktop** (n = 40, p = 0.0017), with participants standing still and rotating their view rather than walking, and no effect of which environment was used ([Krokos et al. PDF](https://obj.umiacs.umd.edu/virtual_reality_study/10.1007-s10055-018-0346-3.pdf)). The authors attribute the gain to vestibular cues from head rotation, which a top-down avatar view cannot supply, so SemPKM should expect its mode to perform like the desktop palaces of Legge and Fassbender, not like VR.

The two recent well-controlled comparisons that pit VR against something cheaper both came back null or negative. Boller, Ouellet and Belleville's randomised trial in 40 older adults found that adding immersive practice to classroom loci training produced **no condition or interaction effect** over an active control ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7994284/)), and Johnsdorf et al. found that plain **text beat both 2D and 360-degree video** for learning rules, proposing that salient VR stimuli pull attention away from the content ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9975255/)). The VR literature also carries a cost 2D does not: a meta-analysis of 55 studies puts mean **dropout at 15.6 percent**, with controller-based locomotion the worst condition ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7145389)). Several palace studies excluded or lost participants to motion sickness, and the only longitudinal in-the-wild study, Vindenes and Wasson's eight weeks with ten students in Hubs, recorded simulator sickness and migraines among the reasons people abandoned their palaces ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-023-00868-y)). A 2D top-down mode trades the small immersion increment for the removal of the field's largest failure mode, which on current evidence is a good trade for something meant to be used daily.

What actually moves recall is controllable in a 2D UI. Reggente et al. gave one group of 30 a single click to "lock" each object into its place and that group recalled **about 28 percent more** than a matched-exposure control; spatial memory for where objects sat predicted verbal recall ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs41465-019-00141-8)). Huttner and colleagues found that fixed, visible loci with item images produced **2.81 times the order-sensitive score** of imagined placement ([HICSS 2018 PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/e95e/e925865c9d999513b7a647b31c4f008ff532.pdf)). Yang et al.'s study of scholarly-abstract recall is instructive in the other direction: the navigable VR coffee shop improved recall with a paired **d = 0.84**, while a static picture-plus-story of the same loci produced **d = 0.058**, essentially nothing, which suggests that a passive, pretty depiction is worthless and that navigability, not realism, is the active ingredient ([TVCG preprint](https://scispace.com/pdf/a-virtual-reality-memory-palace-variant-aids-knowledge-23c63ss5io.pdf)). Ondřej's 2025 meta-analysis of 68 studies extracts the moderators: **dissimilar loci d = 0.91 versus −0.41 for similar loci**, self-generated loci about **d = 0.43** better than assigned ones, roughly two items per locus as the classic recommendation, and a decay in older adults from d = 0.94 immediately to 0.48 at three days; it also rates the overall evidence as very low quality with a Bayes factor for publication bias around 6 × 10⁶ ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12514325/)). Twomey and Kroneisen's smaller meta-analysis of 13 randomised trials lands at g = 0.65 with high risk of bias in most studies ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1747021821993457)). Essoe et al. add the one result that generalises directly to a PKM where knowledge accumulates: learning two similar vocabularies in two distinct VR contexts raised one-week retention from **76 to 92 percent**, but only when participants found the contexts subjectively real ([eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5tv000gq/qt5tv000gq.pdf?t=rxglfz)). Distinct rooms per knowledge set are therefore not decoration; they are the interference control.

The spatial-UI literature from the Data Mountain era tells SemPKM where the ceiling is. Users who arranged **100 web-page thumbnails** on Robertson et al.'s inclined plane retrieved them faster and with fewer errors than from Internet Explorer favourites ([UIST 1998 PDF](https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i247/s04/resources/p153-robertson.pdf)), and when Czerwinski et al. brought them back about **six months later**, retrieval time, errors and failures were unchanged, and removing the thumbnails only briefly slowed them, meaning the arrangement, not the pictures, carried the memory ([INTERACT 1999 PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/1999/01/interact99.pdf)). Cockburn and McKenzie then showed the third dimension was never the reason: across 2,070 trials, retrieval slowed from **3.2 to 4.2 to 5.0 seconds** as density rose from 33 to 99 items, and trended slower with more depth (3.7, 3.8, 4.8 seconds for 2D, 2½D, 3D), with 2D rated least cluttered ([CHI 2002 PDF](https://www.csse.canterbury.ac.nz/andrew.cockburn/papers/chi02DM.pdf)). Jones and Dumais found that location-only reference degraded far faster than name-based reference as item counts grew ([Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/4c5b11cc06e84621618b9240435ffbf4)), and the Scarr, Cockburn and Gutwin synthesis documents how interfaces that rearrange items destroy spatial memory while stable layouts, even when grown incrementally, keep it ([Now Publishers](https://nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadSummary/HCI-046); [Tak et al.](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/improving-window-switching-interfaces/)). Rooms are the natural density cap, stability is non-negotiable, and search and typed relations must remain one click from any placed object.

Two further bodies of work set expectations for engagement and for failure. Sailer and Homner's meta-analysis of gamified learning finds **g = 0.49 for cognitive outcomes**, surviving in high-rigour studies, with "game fiction" and social interaction moderating behavioural effects, which supports a coherent world over bolt-on points ([Augsburg OPUS PDF](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/109056/109056.pdf)). Malone's 1983 desk study found people rely on informal piles for both finding and reminding and find classification hard ([Typeset PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/how-do-people-organize-their-desks-implications-for-the-3j0xdr453p.pdf)), and Marshall and Shipman's spatial-hypertext users expressed relationships through proximity rather than explicit links ([ECHT 1994](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/abstracts/echt94-abstract.html)), so a room can double as a pile the system later parses into typed relationships. The failure mode that matters most for SemPKM is Vindenes and Wasson's: a palace with no text entry, detached from everyday study tools, was abandoned by several of ten students within eight weeks, and those who persisted used it for organising and planning, not for recall drills ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-023-00868-y)). Active navigation helps, but Qin and Karimi's 128 effect sizes show only a small-to-moderate advantage ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17470218231185121)), and Meade et al. argue the benefit comes from decision-making and attention rather than motor effort, with high motor demands sometimes hurting older adults ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6468686)). An avatar walk that forces a choice of door is defensible; one that merely adds travel time is not. The honest gaps are that no controlled study exists of a 2D top-down palace, none tests spaced review inside a palace, none compares user-built with auto-generated layouts head to head, and none evaluates a palace embedded in a working knowledge graph.

## Six rooms users already think in map cleanly onto SemPKM types

Practitioners and historians agree on more than the loose "memory palace" label suggests. The *Rhetorica ad Herennium* already specifies a series of backgrounds "differing in form and nature", of moderate size, neither too bright nor too dim, in a deserted rather than populous region, about **thirty feet apart**, with **every fifth background marked** by a golden hand or an acquaintance named Decimus, and it insists the backgrounds "like wax tablets, should abide" while the images on them change ([UT Austin Memoria](https://laits.utexas.edu/memoria/Ad_Herennium_Passages.html)). Lynne Kelly repeats the rhythm two millennia later, placing a door every fifth location with four items between doors, one item per distinct spot in a fixed order, and reviewing on Dominic O'Brien's schedule of immediately, 24 hours, a week, a month and three months ([Psyche](https://psyche.co/guides/how-to-build-a-memory-palace-to-store-and-revisit-information)). Anthony Metivier's beginner worksheets settle on "10 stations per Memory Palace" with room left to add ten more ([Magnetic Memory Method PDF](https://www.magneticmemorymethod.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Memory_Improvement_Master_Plan_and_Worksheets.pdf)), while forum practitioners report anywhere from three loci per room to ten, with the rule of five being mainly about knowing where each fifth locus is ([Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/10-loci-per-room/30999)). Three constraints recur with near-unanimity: the route should stay open-ended so stations can be appended ([Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/memory-palace-30-day-challenge/50046)); an empty, unfurnished room is a poor palace because its loci are too similar ([Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/minecraft-memory-palace/26328)); and reusing a locus for new material causes "ghosting", so Alex Mullen is reported to use a palace only once when long-term retention matters ([Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/can-i-re-use-the-same-memory-palace-for-different-sets-of-information/86747); [Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/how-do-i-get-rid-of-ghost-images/53388)). The forum material could only be read through search snippets, so treat the specific counts as practitioner opinion rather than consensus; the structural rules are corroborated by the classical and academic sources.

History also hands SemPKM two distinct palace typologies rather than one. The **journey palace** of the *Ad Herennium*, Ricci and Kelly is a linear route through differentiated places, best for sequences and large sets; Kelly describes Aboriginal songlines as "a table of contents to the entire knowledge system" that can be walked physically or in memory ([Big Think](https://bigthink.com/?p=47642)). The **classified theatre** of Camillo and Quiccheberg is a fixed grid where position encodes category: Camillo's wooden theatre divided seven gangways by seven tiers into **49 places**, each with a symbolic figure and "little boxes" of papers beneath, a "windowed mind" meant to hold every human concept ([Socks Studio](https://socks-studio.com/2019/03/03/spatializing-knowledge-giulio-camillos-theatre-of-memory-1519-1544/)). Matteo Ricci's 1596 treatise adds the third, half-real option that SemPKM should adopt as its default authoring story: start from a building you know and "break an imaginary door" through the back wall into new space ([Magnetic Memory Method](https://www.magneticmemorymethod.com/?p=4980)). A room template library should therefore offer both a gallery corridor (route, numbered loci) and a cabinet room (N × M shelf grid), and the main corridor of a palace should itself be a navigable index of the rooms. Chiang and Bakkour's Minecraft study is a useful footnote on mood: a gloomy, overgrown palace produced better recall than a bright, flowered one, and both beat no palace ([UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/how-build-better-memory-palace)), so the art direction need not be relentlessly cosy.

Modern PKM does not literally name rooms, but it zones consistently. Johnny Decimal's official analogy is that "a computer is a room, an area is a filing cabinet, a category is a drawer, and an ID is a manila folder", with hard limits of ten areas and ten categories ([Johnny Decimal](https://johnnydecimal.com/documentation/introduction)); PARA sorts by actionability into projects, areas, resources and archives; and Notion "second brain" guides centre on a capture inbox and warn that systems stall when people "capture but never process" ([Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/second-brain-notion/)). Zettelkasten is the dissenter: Luhmann held that "the actual place is of lesser importance and what matters is that you can find something when you need it", and its Folgezettel numbering places a new card behind its parent rather than in a topical room ([Zettelkasten forum](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/24851)), which maps onto shelf order within a room, not onto separate rooms. The only two note-to-geometry mappings found in the wild agree with each other: the Obsidian plugin KMind Palace binds a note to a piece of furniture or a single book, lets a bookshelf mirror a folder, puts palaces on zoomable islands, uses FSRS spaced repetition and lets neglected furniture gather cobwebs ([Obsidian plugin page](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/kmind-palace)), and Mine Palace, a 2026 hackathon project, turns top-level folders into districts around a hub, dated journal files into year, month and day alcoves, and notes into books on chiselled shelves ([Mine Palace](https://minepalace.vipul.xyz/); [write-up](https://vipul.xyz/2026/08/15/minepalace/)). Both independently chose library furniture for notes and time-indexed alcoves for journals. The commercial lesson is sharper still: every palace product that gained traction shipped pre-built furnished spaces, from memoryOS's "850+ loci" and "each palace room is a folder in your mind" ([memoryOS](https://memoryos.com/)) to Macunx's quincunx pedestal arrays, while the blank-canvas Munx VR builder sits at 18 Steam reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/871490)), and memoryOS users still complain that "navigation of the memory palace module" is unclear and frustrating ([appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/1553283646/reviews/5)). Furnished templates matter more than a flexible editor, and a 2D map with an always-visible minimap avoids the 3D navigation complaint.

Putting these together yields a default mapping from SemPKM's typed objects to rooms and connectors. The six zones users already think in (inbox, projects, areas, resources, archive, home) are the safest default rooms; thematic rooms such as a study, lab or garden are design choices with no mnemonic tradition behind them and should be optional templates.

| SemPKM object type or structure | Default room | Loci and ordering | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inbox, fleeting notes, unprocessed captures | Entrance hall or mailroom, first room inside the door | Items visibly pile up; walk begins by processing them | Notion inbox-first guidance ([Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/second-brain-notion/)) |
| Sources, papers, books, ebooks, videos, literature notes | Library | Shelves of ~10; literature note shelved beside its source; one shelf per medium | KMind and Mine Palace shelves ([KMind](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/kmind-palace); [Mine Palace](https://minepalace.vipul.xyz/)); Metivier's 10-shelf bookcases |
| Permanent notes, Zettelkasten chains | Study or scriptorium | Ordered along the shelf by Folgezettel (behind the parent), a corridor of sequence | Luhmann numbering ([Zettelkasten forum](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/24851)) |
| Concepts from the ontology, taxonomies | Gallery or cabinet room | N × M grid; cell position encodes class; image on the surface, text "in the box beneath" | Camillo's 49 places ([Socks Studio](https://socks-studio.com/2019/03/03/spatializing-knowledge-giulio-camillos-theatre-of-memory-1519-1544/)) |
| Projects and their tasks | Workshop, one room per project | Loci are the project's stages or tasks in order, with an unused exit for growth | Chen's "one stage per room" ([Mullen Memory](https://mullenmemory.com/memory-palace)) |
| Events, journals, dated notes | Clock tower or calendar cloister | Regularly spaced alcoves by year, month, day | Mine Palace diary mode ([Mine Palace](https://minepalace.vipul.xyz/)) |

| Archived objects, retired palaces | Vault or archive wing | Rooms are archived whole, never cleared and refilled | Mullen's use-once rule against ghosting |

Connectors should be typed too, because SemPKM's relationships are. Doors carry containment and hierarchy (Ricci's door knocked through the back wall); corridors carry sequence (Folgezettel chains, project stages); stairs carry abstraction level, following Camillo's tiers rising from the elemental to the arts; and windows carry weak cross-references, places you can see into but not walk through, the literal *mens fenestrata*. The outdoor path between buildings is Metivier's inter-palace journey and Kelly's songline, which makes a multi-palace instance a town rather than a single sprawling house. Density defaults follow the five-ten cadence: five loci in a small room, ten in a large one, a visible marker (door, statue, numbered floor tile) every fifth locus, one object per locus, and a soft warning when a room passes about a dozen placements. Two contradictions with practitioner advice should be designed around rather than ignored. First, loci are for ordered material, so an unordered typed room loses the method's main power; the fix is to let every room expose an editable route (a numbered path) that the avatar can follow, and to treat the room's default order (creation date, Folgezettel, project stage) as that route unless the user redraws it. Second, Chen and Mullen only palace things they "want to say fluently", not lists they can write down ([Mullen Memory](https://mullenmemory.com/memory-palace)); most PKM content is the latter, which is why the palace must be positioned as a re-encounter and overview surface over the graph, with recall drill as an optional mode for the subset of objects the user deliberately pins.

## Approach, prompt, panel: three arrival modes converged on independently

Three unrelated lineages arrived at the same grammar for what happens when an avatar reaches a place: approach, see an affordance, press a key, get a panel. WorkAdventure attaches typed properties to rectangular areas in a Tiled map, and `openWebsite` opens the URL as an iframe docked to the right of the screen, with `openWebsiteTrigger` choosing between opening as soon as the avatar enters, `onaction` (a bottom-of-screen "Press on SPACE to open the web site" message) and `onicon` (a passive icon the user may click), plus `openTab` to escape to a new browser tab and `openWebsiteClosable` to control dismissal ([WorkAdventure docs](https://docs.workadventu.re/map-building/tiled-editor/opening-a-website)). Its scripting API caps the co-website at **70 percent of the viewport**, offers `WA.ui.openPopup` for a speech-bubble popup with buttons anchored to a map rectangle, `WA.ui.displayActionMessage` for the SPACE-dismissed prompt, `WA.ui.modal.openModal` for a left, centre or right iframe panel, and `WA.room.website.create` for the rarer case of an iframe rendered at world coordinates inside the map ([api-nav](https://docs.workadventu.re/developer/map-scripting/references/api-nav); [api-ui](https://docs.workadventu.re/developer/map-scripting/references/api-ui)). Gather's model is a 32-pixel grid where decorative objects do nothing and interactive objects show `Press "x" to interact.` within activation distance, swap to an "active image" when the avatar is close, and on x open an embedded website, image, video or note in an iframe inside the world ([Gather objects](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15910376994708); [Gather advanced options](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15910418059796)); space-level behaviour comes from five tile effects, Impassable, Spawn, Portal, Private Area and Spotlight ([Gather tile effects](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15910385115412)). Hobbyist RPG portfolio sites rediscover the same thing: walk to an NPC, get a bottom-of-screen dialog box, optionally jump to a conventional project panel, and one author keeps a plain portfolio alongside because visitors use it "99% of the time when they just want to quickly see my work" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/svet_62385e9/svetlinas-quest-a-gamified-portfolio-14bh)). That last remark is the single most important product constraint: the palace is an alternate view over the same graph, never the only way to reach content.

For SemPKM this means a per-place arrival setting with three modes, applied at two granularities. A **room** (a set of tiles sharing an id, structurally identical to Gather's Private Area) carries an ambient response on entry: the camera settles, the room's name and object count are announced, the lighting or music changes, and at most a compact, non-blocking strip appears. A **locus** (a furniture object with a placed SemPKM object) carries an opt-in response: a highlight at activation distance, a prompt, and on Enter the object opens. Entering the Study should therefore not slam a dashboard over the map; it should make the desk, pinboard and lamp readable at a glance and show a "Press Enter at the desk to open your research board" affordance, so the walk ends with a choice rather than an interruption. Pressing Enter at the desk opens the research dashboard as a compact overlay (the next section defines its tiers); pressing Enter again, or choosing "open full", drops to the real GridStack dashboard in the side panel or a new tab, which is exactly WorkAdventure's `openTab` and Gather's "sites that block framing open in a new tab" fallback ([Gather embedded websites](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15910417713940)). Entering the Library should show the shelves themselves as the collection, with the reading chair as the "currently consuming" locus and a returns cart for items to drop; pressing Enter at a shelf opens that shelf's row as a list, at a book opens the object, and at the lectern opens the full collection view with sorting and filtering.

The cozy "productivity game" category confirms the market tolerates and perhaps expects this hybrid. A February 2026 survey of Steam identified **34 productivity games, nine with more than 500 reviews and three past one million dollars**, led by Spirit City: Lofi Sessions, with a common feature set of pomodoro timer, task list and focus audio ([Substack](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/the-rise-of-productivity-games-on)). Spirit City, Overwhelmingly Positive with roughly 8,800 reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2113850/Spirit_City_Lofi_Sessions/)), keeps its avatar moving between fireplace, window seat and desk while the to-do list, timer, habit tracker and journal stay as conventional panels on the right of the screen ([LadiesGamers](https://ladiesgamers.com/spirit-city-lofi-sessions-review/)). None of these products drives room-object state from a knowledge graph, and none found makes walking rather than clicking the trigger; that is the niche SemPKM would occupy. Two older precedents sharpen what "tight" mapping means. psDooM renders Unix processes as Doom monsters where shooting renices and killing sends `kill -9` ([psDooM README](https://psdoom.sourceforge.net/docs/README)); the mapping is memorable because the verb matches the effect. Hades splits progress from reading: the Codex notifies in the HUD whenever an entry advances, but the Mirror of Night is a full-screen upgrade grid you reach by walking to a physical mirror in Zagreus's bedroom ([Medium](https://medium.com/@Nat.Rowley/how-hades-creates-a-responsive-underworld-915715a7c2a); [Fextralife](https://hades.wiki.fextralife.com/Mirror+of+the+Night)). SemPKM should do the same: progress and due-ness surface in the world, reading and editing happen through a door the user chooses to open.

Arrival should also be where the palace writes back to the graph. Each placement should be a command through `POST /api/commands` that creates a `palace:Placement` linking the object IRI to a room and tile, so that "where is this object" and "what is in the Library" are plain SPARQL, and so that the pin action itself is the deliberate binding Reggente's click-to-lock showed is worth about 28 percent ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs41465-019-00141-8)). Because every write is already an immutable event in a named graph, placement history, undo and the "this locus previously held X" warning against ghosting come for free from the event store. Text entry inside the palace is not optional: Vindenes and Wasson's abandonment data trace directly to its absence ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-023-00868-y)), so the locus overlay must allow creating a note, adding a comment and editing a title without leaving the world.

## Sprites carry coarse state, overlays carry reading, GridStack carries editing

Game UI theory gives SemPKM a precise vocabulary for "RPG-style, diegetic" display. Fagerholt and Lorentzon's 2009 taxonomy crosses two questions, whether the element exists in the fiction and whether it exists in the game space, producing diegetic (in both: Dead Space's spine-mounted health bar), meta (in the fiction but on the screen plane: blood splatter), spatial (in the space but not the fiction: Fable's glowing trail) and non-diegetic (the classic HUD) ([DICE slides](https://www2.slideshare.net/DICEStudio/beyond-the-hud-user-interfaces-for-increased-player-immersion-in-fps-games); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/user-interface-design-in-video-games)). For a palace, diegetic means the room *is* the dashboard (shelf fullness is the backlog), spatial means badges anchored to objects (a "3" over the mailbox), meta means a screen tint (a sepia wash over a stale project), and non-diegetic means the GridStack dashboard. The empirical record is small and mostly first-person shooters, but it is consistent on one point: legibility of the encoding matters more than where it sits. Peacocke et al.'s 20-player lab study found the diegetic *numeric* ammo display beat HUD icons, with **35 percent fewer dry-fire shots, 26 percent faster reloads and 70 percent preference**, and their review of earlier work records players finding vague diegetic displays disruptive and valuing clear communication over realism ([GEM 2015 PDF](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf)). A Jyväskylä thesis found strong evidence diegetic interfaces were harder to use and insufficient evidence they improved immersion ([JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/56744)), and a 39-participant VR study concluded a traditional HUD was more efficient for information access ([IJHCI 2021](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2020.1870819)). Marcus Andrews's DICE essay drew the practical conclusion fifteen years ago: fully in-world UI is hard, Team Fortress 2 proves players tolerate mixed layers, and World of Warcraft's customisable HUD shows experts want density ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-ui-discoveries-what-players-want)). Offer a toggle; do not force full diegesis.

Within that constraint, the encoding rules are well established. Adams's heuristics, as applied by Peacocke et al., say to use digits for quantities you would write as numbers, bars or fullness for glanceable state, and countable icon rows only up to about five items ([GEM 2015 PDF](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf)). Thirty years of ambient-display research supplies the rest. Weiser and Brown's calm technology "moves back and forth between the centre and the periphery of attention", exemplified by the Dangling String whose spin rate tracked network traffic ([calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology)); Tomitsch et al.'s survey of 51 ambient systems adopts the five-step notification ladder of **ignore, change blind, make aware, interrupt, demand attention**, pairs low levels with slow transitions and high levels with abrupt ones, and catalogues encodings such as the History Tablecloth's halo that grows the longer an object is left, the Flashbag USB stick that inflates as it fills, and glow intensity for probability ([Tomitsch et al. PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf)). Jansen, Dragicevic and colleagues define a data physicalization as an artifact "whose geometry or material properties encode data" and argue the familiar and tangible draws people in ([CHI 2015 PDF](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/72691/1/OA_2015_04_CHI_DataPhys.pdf)); Miller and Stasko's InfoCanvas, a user-designed pictorial scene whose elements are bound to data feeds, is almost a description of a palace room ([Georgia Tech slides](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/gvu/ii/talks/chi01.pdf)). Cozy games have already solved the pixel-art versions: Animal Crossing perches a small yellow bird on the bulletin board when there is an unread notice ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Yellow_Bird)) and its Critterpedia marks donated items with an owl icon ([Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Critterpedia)); Stardew Valley's collections grey out known-but-uncooked recipes and show unknown ones as "???" ([Stardew Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Collections)); The Sims collapses six need bars into a green, yellow or red plumbob ([Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Plumbob)); Dead Space steps its health bar through aqua, green, yellow and blinking red at 75, 50 and 25 percent ([Dead Space Wiki](https://deadspace.fandom.com/wiki/Resource_Integration_Gear)).

| Data shape | Sprite channel (Tier 0) | Notification level | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boolean (unread exists, something due) | Presence of a small creature or lit candle | Make aware | Animal Crossing's yellow bird |
| Ordinal, three to four steps (staleness, health) | Colour step or sprite variant: fresh, droopy, brown, dead plant | Change blind | Plumbob, Dead Space RIG |
| Small count (up to five) | Countable items: letters in the mailbox, books on the desk | Change blind | Adams's icon-row limit |
| Large count (backlog, collection size) | Container fullness in four or five frames, plus a hover digit | Ignore to change blind | Flashbag, Stardew shipping bin |
| Rate (activity volume, streak) | Animation speed: hearth flames, spinning string | Ignore | Dangling String |
| Elapsed time (days since touched) | Accumulation: dust, cobwebs, halo, stack of newspapers | Change blind | History Tablecloth, KMind's cobwebs |
| Ratio or progress | In-fiction meter: hourglass, candle length, water in a jar | Change blind | Ambient progress displays |
| Hard deadline, must acknowledge | NPC approaches holding a scroll, speech bubble | Interrupt | Hello.Wall, Nabaztag |

Concretely, the Study becomes a desk whose paper stack height is the active project's open tasks, a pinboard of the six most recent notes with tack colour by type, a lamp that is lit when something is due today and flickers when overdue, a wall map with pins for the concepts touched this week, a plant that wilts by days since the last edit, and a cat asleep on the desk when nothing has happened for a week. The Library becomes one shelf per medium with spine width for length, full colour for unread and faded grey for consumed (Stardew's greyed-recipe pattern), a bookmark ribbon for in progress, dust on a shelf section untouched for thirty days, and a Critterpedia-style wall chart with silhouettes for an unread curriculum. The bookshelf literature warns what not to do: Apple dropped iBooks' wood veneer in iOS 7 as "visual noise" that "doesn't really work like a bookshelf" ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/13/11/14/apple-updates-ibooks-itunes-u-with-fresh-flatter-looks-for-ios-7); [AppleInsider forum](https://forums.appleinsider.com/discussion/152504)), yet library-science work keeps rebuilding shelves because users say digital libraries have "no digital analogue of library shelves" ([City University PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12536/1/McKayBuchananChangCameraReady.pdf)) and the Blended Shelf field study confirmed shelf browsing matters when the shelf carries real attributes like size and availability ([KOPS Konstanz PDF](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/245c5a47-4238-40a9-aa35-3b02e2f34d8f/content)). The shelf survives as information (adjacency, thickness, gaps, order) and dies as ornament. Two further lessons come from games: Moonlighter's notebook only recorded perfect-price sales and players distrusted it as "flawed" ([Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2018/11/05/review-moonlighter-on-switch/)), so every sprite state must be derived live from the graph with a visible "as of", never from a separate store; and Stardew's shipping bin confuses new players because it has no label ([Stardew Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Shipping_Box)), so every interactive object needs a spatial label or signpost until used once. Unpacking's developers, who told a story through about a thousand placed household items, call objects "blunt and imprecise tools" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items)), which is the right expectation: encode only coarse states in sprites and leave fine values to digits and the overlay.

The transition problem, when to leave the fiction, resolves into three tiers. **Tier 0, glance**: sprite states, spatial badges and a hover tooltip carrying the exact digit, always visible, never pausing. **Tier 1, the lectern overlay**: a pixel-framed HTML modal anchored to the object that renders a compact htmx fragment, the top five due items, one shelf row, one codex page, with large type, kept to one screen, and, following Dead Space's holographic inventory that "does not pause gameplay" ([Dead Space Wiki](https://deadspace.fandom.com/wiki/Resource_Integration_Gear)), left non-modal so ambient animation continues. Only the frame is pixel-styled; the content is real DOM, because canvas-rendered text panels fail readability and accessibility and double the maintenance surface, and because libraries such as NES.css (pure CSS 8-bit components, Press Start 2P) and pixel-ui-dialog (MIT dialog boxes in plain HTML and CSS) make a 9-slice pixel border a few lines of CSS ([NES.css](https://github.com/shtakai/NES.css); [pixel-ui-dialog](https://github.com/browsermage/pixel-ui-dialog)). **Tier 2, the real UI**: the existing GridStack DashboardSpec renderer or object tab in the side panel or a new tab, the Gather "press x, iframe" pattern, for anything that needs sorting, filtering, editing or SPARQL. The fallback rules are simple to state: open Tier 1 whenever the user must read more than about five items or any body text; open Tier 2 whenever they must sort, filter, edit or query; always offer a non-diegetic list-view toggle for experts and assistive technology. The main evidence gap here is that no study tests diegetic *dashboards* (lists, counts, queues) in 2D games at all; the cheapest way to close it is a short Wizard-of-Oz test asking the same questions of a static mock room and the GridStack dashboard.

## Share the structure and the snapshot first; live presence is a late add-on

SemPKM already shares named graphs between instances through LDN invitations and RDF Patch sync, so the question is what a palace adds on top. The precedents answer with two separate axes that every product keeps apart: who may enter and what they may change. Animal Crossing lets up to eight players visit, but regular visitors cannot use an axe or shovel and only "Best Friends" can use tools, see when you are online and message you ([Nintendo UK](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2020/May/Top-tips-for-playing-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons-with-friends-1783708.html)). Stardew's save lives on the host, farmhands cannot enter when the host is offline, and farmhouse upgrades, cabin demolition, pausing and kick or ban are host-only ([Stardew Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer)). VRChat runs eight instance tiers from Public through Friends+, Friends, Invite+ and Invite to Group variants, with the instance owner moderating in all but Public ([VRChat Wiki](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Instances)), and Gather layers space passwords, email-domain restriction and guest lists while members bypass all of them ([Gather help](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15975751960340)). Historically, palaces are shared the same way: forum practitioners trade journeys "if the other person's memory palace is compatible with your system" ([Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/shared-mnemonic-journeys-using-anothers-memory-journey/56109)), Macunx explicitly advertised a mode to "build palaces for others to use" ([LS:N Global](https://www.lsnglobal.com/news/article/19825/virtual-memory)), and Camillo built his theatre for patrons. The structure (floor plan, route, shelf grid, walking direction, loci count) is what gets shared; the images and contents stay personal. For SemPKM this argues for shareable room templates and routes as the first sharing primitive, with per-user contents, and for the Sims Gallery's third mode of export-and-fork a layout decoupled from private content.

The lowest-risk form of visiting is the one people actually use to show off a space. Animal Crossing's dream islands upload a snapshot that others wander "even while they are not playing"; visitors cannot change anything, their inventory is removed on entry, nothing they do is saved, the host may update the snapshot once a day, Luna "warns you about public data" before upload, and the owner can delete the Dream Address at any time, after which it stops working ([Nintendo support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-(animal-crossing:-new-horizons)); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/683468/how-to-visit-someones-island-in-dreams-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons/)). Reporters Without Borders' Uncensored Library in Minecraft, about 300 banned articles across twelve wings, is readable by everyone on the server but immutable ([RSF](https://rsf.org/en/news/rsf-opens-uncensored-library-digital-home-press-freedom-within-global-computer-game)). The mapping onto SemPKM is exact: a room's visibility is the visibility of the named graph it exhibits. "Publish this gallery" means binding the room's `palace:Placement` triples and the map blob to a `urn:sempkm:shared:{uuid}` graph and inviting WebIDs or federation partners through the existing LDN flow; the consent dialog can then be more precise than Luna's warning because it can list the graphs being exposed. Visitors on another instance render the snapshot from synced data with no live connection to the owner's server, and revocation is removing the room from the shared graph. Publishing rooms rather than isolated objects also answers Joel Chan's critique of shared knowledge graphs, that excerpted ideas lose value without their surrounding connections and that sharing thoughts carries harm risks ([Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1309521782806847490)); the room carries its context with it, and digital-garden theory says that topology, visible work-in-progress and explicit maturity status are precisely what make a personal knowledge space worth wandering ([Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history)). The strongest value proposition in the sources is relational: visitors come to see how a person arranges and annotates, not to retrieve facts, so a shared palace should foreground the owner's arrangement over a generic graph view.

Asynchronous presence is where the controlled evidence is best, and it needs no infrastructure. Hirsch et al. put 26 participants in a virtual living room with and without traces of use and found social presence **significantly higher with traces (p = .003)**, more and longer looks at objects, and about thirty seconds longer stays, with twelve participants calling the traced room "homely" and "authentic" and six preferring the tidy one ([IEEE VR 2022 PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/hirsch_ieeeVR22/hirsch_ieeeVR22.pdf)). The Souls games show how to bound such traces: players leave messages "using fixed templates and a limited vocabulary", bloodstains replay as non-interactive ghosts that cannot affect your world, and the result is "anonymous camaraderie" ([arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.10949)); Death Stranding adds likes on structures and paths that "smooth over in time" as people walk them ([Death Stranding wiki](https://deathstranding.fandom.com/wiki/Social_Strand_System)); Wexelblat and Maes's Footprints showed interaction history let web users do the same work "with significantly less effort" ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/29146)). Journey's designers removed names, chat and visible gear because names leaked identity and gear "created visible, often times impenetrable hierarchies" ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/)). SemPKM should let visitors leave template-constrained notes and appreciation on loci, store them as ordinary RDF comments in the shared graph so they federate and survive (Demon's Souls lost every message when its servers closed), run them through the existing comment moderation, and show a worn-path heatmap of which exhibits visitors actually walked to. Hirsch's twelve-to-six split means traces must be confinable to public rooms or switchable to a tidy view.

Live co-presence should wait, and when it comes it should be text-only. The classic CSCW tradeoff of Hudson and Smith is that more awareness means less privacy for the observed and more disruption for the observer ([CSCW '96 PDF](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Hudson-CSCW96-p248-hudson.pdf)), and Boyle, Edwards and Greenberg showed blur-level abstraction preserves awareness while safeguarding privacy ([PRISM](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/items/329fd90e-197e-47b0-8087-cb2e73b15465/full)); so "who is in my palace now" should default to counts and ghost avatars, with names only for mutually connected WebIDs and a solitude mode for visitors, since reading someone's knowledge is sensitive. Williamson et al.'s instrumented Mozilla Hubs workshop found smaller rooms produced more cohesive groups while large rooms made grouping harder ([arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.05300)), and conference organisers never saw more than about 150 people in Gather against far larger Zoom plenaries ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08795)), so a palace visit is inherently a small-group affair and room sizes should match. The business history is cautionary but not disqualifying: Gather cut about a third of its staff in mid-2022 ([Forbes repost](https://kuangkunang.blogspot.com/2022/07/virtual-office-startup-gather-laid-off.html)), Teamflow and Wonder shut, Skittish closed when its spatial-audio API vendor gave six weeks' notice ([waxy.org](https://waxy.org/2022/10/winding-down-skittish)), and Mozilla ended Hubs on 31 May 2024 ([Mozilla](https://hubs.mozilla.com/labs/sunset)). Everything expensive in those stacks was media: WorkAdventure's self-hosting guide requires LiveKit for group video, Coturn because "about 15% of users may fail to establish audio/video" without it, and optional Jitsi and Matrix, across three servers with public IPs ([WorkAdventure install doc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/workadventure/workadventure/develop/docs/others/self-hosting/install.md)), and its code is under AGPL with a Commons Clause that is not OSI-approved ([WorkAdventure FAQ](https://workadventu.re/faq/what-is-the-license-of-the-self-hosted-version-of-workadventure/)). Presence-only sync, by contrast, is one FastAPI WebSocket route with the documented in-memory `ConnectionManager`, which the FastAPI docs note works for a single process and can be backed by `encode/broadcaster` over Redis for more ([FastAPI docs](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/advanced/websockets/)); clients send tile moves at about 10 Hz and interpolate, roughly half Colyseus's 20 Hz default ([Colyseus docs](https://docs.colyseus.io/)). Cross-instance live presence would need a cross-origin WebSocket with WebID auth and is real design work; asynchronous snapshots and traces travel as graph data and avoid it entirely.

## melonJS or hand-rolled Canvas, a TMJ subset, CC0 16-pixel art and a DOM twin

The engine decision is between two defensible options and several poor fits. Phaser 4 is the most mature full engine, with v4.0.0 shipped 10 April 2026 and patch releases through July ([Phaser releases](https://github.com/phaserjs/phaser/releases)), but its minified bundle is **1.27 to 1.38 MB** ([unpkg](https://app.unpkg.com/phaser@4.2.1/files/dist)), heavy for a mode inside a PKM tab. PixiJS v8 is a renderer rather than an engine: about **244 KB gzipped** for the core ([depscope](https://depscope.dev/pkg/npm/pixi.js)) before adding the separate `@pixi/tilemap` plugin, which does not parse Tiled files ([pixijs/tilemap](https://github.com/pixijs/tilemap)), a camera, input or a game loop. Excalibur is BSD-2 with an official Tiled plugin but releases roughly quarterly ([Excalibur Tiled plugin](https://excaliburjs.com/docs/tiled-plugin)); Kaplay's v4000 line has been in alpha for over a year ([Kaplay blog](https://kaplay4000.pages.dev/blog/release-v4000-alpha-26)); Godot's web export was about 40 MB uncompressed and 5 MB Brotli at 4.3, and threaded builds require cross-origin-isolation headers that would apply to the whole SemPKM origin and break every third-party iframe elsewhere in the app ([Godot 4.3 progress report](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/godot/comments/1cso7zx/progress_report_web_export_in_43); [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). That leaves **melonJS**, MIT, "~250 KB minzipped", zero dependencies, tree-shakeable ESM targeting ES2022, built-in Tiled support to format 1.12, SAT collision with a quadtree, and a multi-camera system with follow deadzone, releasing roughly weekly through October 2026 with 14 open issues ([melonJS](https://github.com/melonjs/melonJS)), and a **hand-rolled Canvas 2D renderer**, which for an orthogonal map of 36 × 20 to 100 × 100 tiles, a handful of sprites and collision-only physics is plausibly 10 to 30 KB of application code with no dependency, matching the approach of SemPKM's existing `canvas.js` spatial canvas. The recommendation is to start hand-rolled, because the rendering is trivial and the hard parts (overlay, graph binding, editor) are not engine problems, and to keep the map file Tiled-compatible so melonJS or Phaser can be swapped in if animation or physics ambitions grow. The DOM is not an option at the upper end: the available evidence says DOM-based canvases "start lagging around 1,000 elements" during zoom ([thebiggish report](https://thebiggish.pages.dev/news/developer-pushes-pixi-js-infinite-canvas-to-60-000-cards-before-performance-breaks)) and canvas is "the clear winner" at hundreds to thousands of objects ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/when-to-use-html5s-canvas-ce992b100ee8)), and a 100 × 100 map is 10,000 tiles per layer, though no rigorous DOM-versus-canvas tilemap benchmark was found.

The map format should be a strict subset of Tiled's JSON. TMJ gives tile layers (CSV or base64 with zlib, gzip or zstd), object layers with points, tile objects, polygons and text, typed custom properties including `object` references and `class` structs, templates, tilesets with per-tile collision shapes and animations, and Wang sets for corner, edge or mixed autotiling ([Tiled JSON format](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/reference/json-map-format/)). WorkAdventure demonstrates a complete "behaviour via properties" convention on top of it: orthogonal 32-pixel maps with embedded tilesets, a mandatory `floorLayer` object group so everything above it draws over the avatar, `collides` set on tileset tiles, a `start` layer for spawns and `exitUrl` layers for exits ([WorkAdventure maps](https://docs.workadventu.re/map-building/tiled-editor/wa-maps); [entry/exit](https://docs.workadventu.re/map-building/tiled-editor/entry-exit)). SemPKM's palace file is then an orthogonal map with two to four tile layers (floor, walls and furniture, over-avatar), one `objectgroup` of placements whose properties carry `sempkm:iri`, `sempkm:type` and label, and `class`-typed room metadata, so power users can round-trip through desktop Tiled. Persistence should be hybrid: the tile layers are a JSON blob written through the command API (a 36 × 20 map with three layers is 6 to 10 KB as CSV; 100 × 100 is 90 to 150 KB, far less compressed), while rooms, placements, doors and routes are RDF (`palace:Room`, `palace:Placement`, `palace:Door`, `palace:Route`) so they are queryable and federate; 30,000 cells as triples would be a graph explosion for data with no semantics beyond rendering. WorkAdventure stores maps as flat `.tmj` files behind a thin map-storage service, which confirms the blob approach scales ([WorkAdventure Tiled editor](https://docs.workadventu.re/map-building/tiled-editor/)). No mature, permissively licensed in-browser tile editor with autotiling exists; LDtk is MIT but Electron-only ([deepnight/ldtk](https://github.com/deepnight/ldtk)) and the best vanilla-JS reference is blurymind's dependency-free editor with layers, bucket fill and undo ([blurymind/tilemap-editor](https://github.com/blurymind/tilemap-editor)), so the in-app editor will be built in-house: palette painting, rectangle and flood fill, eraser, layers, undo via inverse patches, and Wang-set autotiling driven by the tileset's own `wangsets`. Given the commercial lesson that furnished templates beat blank canvases, the editor's first job is placing and reshaping pre-furnished room templates, not painting tiles.

Art licensing is the trap to avoid early. The clean path is CC0 16-pixel art: Kenney's Roguelike/RPG Pack (2015, tagged furniture, town, rpg) and Tiny Town (2023) ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/roguelike-rpg-pack); [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/tiny-town)), Pixel Boy's Ninja Adventure with interior elements, 50-plus animated characters, two fonts and an update in March 2026 ([itch.io](https://pixel-boy.itch.io/ninja-adventure-asset-pack)), and 0x72's DungeonTileset II for anything crypt-like ([itch.io](https://0x72.itch.io/dungeontileset-ii)). Limezu's Modern Interiors is the only surveyed set that clearly covers libraries, classrooms and offices at 16, 32 and 48 pixels, but it forbids redistribution and requires credit ([itch.io](https://limezu.itch.io/moderninteriors)), so an installable app could not ship it and could only tell users where to drop their own copy. The Liberated Pixel Cup assets are rich in furniture but CC-BY-SA 3.0 or GPL 3.0 with per-file credits ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/liberated-pixel-cup-lpc-base-assets-sprites-map-tiles)), and the Universal LPC character generator, the only free browser-based avatar generator found, is GPL-3.0 code over mixed-licence art that exports a credits CSV per sheet ([GitHub](https://github.com/LiberatedPixelCup/Universal-LPC-Spritesheet-Character-Generator)). Type colouring should not depend on recolouring sprites at all; a type-coloured base plate, outline or badge under the sprite is licence-neutral and keeps the "no essential information by colour alone" rule satisfiable with a shape. Rendering must be integer-scaled: MDN's guidance is integer multiples with `image-rendering: pixelated`, which distorts at non-integer scales ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games/Techniques/Crisp_pixel_art_look)), so draw the world to an offscreen canvas at native 16-pixel resolution, blit at 2×, 3× or 4× chosen from the container, snap positions to whole native pixels, and put all labels in an absolutely positioned HTML layer so text stays vector-sharp and readable by assistive technology.

Accessibility for a canvas world has no WCAG recipe, so the Game Accessibility Guidelines and Xbox Accessibility Guidelines are the applicable checklists: remappable controls, the same input method for UI and gameplay, no essential information by colour or sound alone, high contrast and adjustable text, no flicker, an option to disable background movement, and screen-reader support ([GAG full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/); [XAG](https://learn.microsoft.com/gaming/accessibility/guidelines)). The practical pattern, an inference rather than a documented guideline, is a parallel DOM twin: a "room list" mode that lists rooms and their placed objects as real links reusing SemPKM's object tabs, teleport-to-room from that list so walking is optional, an `aria-live="polite"` region announcing "Entered Library, four objects nearby" on room entry and the object name on facing it, arrow or WASD plus Enter and Escape as the entire control scheme, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured by snapping the camera and disabling idle animation and any shake, fade or flash. Because Tier 1 overlays are real HTML, they inherit focus order, zoom and screen-reader semantics for free, which is one more reason not to draw panels on the canvas.

## Prioritised recommendations and an initial feature set

The recommendations below are ordered by the strength of the evidence behind them and by how much each unlocks. The first group is non-negotiable because it is where the recall evidence lives; the second builds the engagement and overview value that will actually sustain daily use; the third is sharing; the fourth is deferred.

Package the mode as a Mental Model plus a platform app. The palace ontology (`palace:Room`, `palace:Placement`, `palace:Door`, `palace:Route`, `palace:Template`) with SHACL shapes and the default dashboards belongs in a Mental Model installed from the catalog, while the renderer, editor and WebSocket presence route belong in an `apps/palace/manifest.yaml` subprocess with custom pages. This keeps the palace a view over the graph, honours the "keep a boring version too" rule by construction, and lets every placement flow through `POST /api/commands` into the event store so undo, history and ghosting warnings are free.

The initial release should be the P0 row set plus the Study and Library sprite channels from P1, which is enough to test the two user stories in the brief end to end: walk into the Study and see the research state at a glance, press Enter at the desk for the compact board and again for the full dashboard; walk into the Library and see the consumption queue on the shelves, press Enter at the chair for what is in progress and at the lectern for the full collection. Instrument from day one: time-to-object through the palace versus through search, revisit frequency per room, pins per room, and whether users ever open Tier 2 from a locus, because these are the measurements no published study has made. Run the Wizard-of-Oz comparison of a static mock room against the GridStack dashboard before investing in more sprite channels, and plan a small within-subject recall test (pinned objects versus unpinned, lenient and strict scoring) once the pin action ships, since the absence of any 2D top-down palace study means SemPKM would be generating, not consuming, the evidence.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what SemPKM is building. The memory palace of competition mnemonists, where a disposable list is burned into a once-used route, is a poor model for permanent, accumulating knowledge; the better ancestors are Camillo's windowed theatre with papers in boxes beneath each image and the medieval cloister that one lives in and revisits. The evidence that matters most is not the VR effect size but the Data Mountain's six-month retention of a hundred self-arranged positions and Reggente's 28 percent from a single deliberate click: a 2D map that the user builds, never reflows, and binds objects to with intent is already on the right side of everything that is known, and the mode's real promise is that SemPKM's typed graph can make the room itself truthful in ways no cozy productivity game has attempted, with shelf fullness, wilting plants and dust derived from SPARQL rather than hand-set.

That same framing sets the honest limit. Nobody has measured a 2D top-down palace, a palace inside a working knowledge graph, or spaced review inside a palace, and the one longitudinal study shows people walk away from a palace that is not where their work happens. The mode will succeed or fail on whether the Study is a faster, more pleasant way to reach the research dashboard than the sidebar, not on whether users memorise more; the recall bonus, if it appears, will come from the small set of objects people choose to pin and walk past again. Building the palace as a Mental Model and platform app over the command API, with the GridStack dashboard one keypress away and a DOM twin for everyone the canvas excludes, is what turns a charming demo into a second retrieval path that can earn its place in daily use.
