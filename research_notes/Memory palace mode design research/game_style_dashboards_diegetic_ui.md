# Game-style and diegetic dashboards: rendering SemPKM information displays inside a 2D top-down "memory palace" level

Research date: 2026-11-03. Scope: how lists, counts, progress, timelines, backlogs, reading queues, recent activity, due items and relationships can be shown as in-world objects in a 2D RPG room, when to fall back to an overlay, and what theory, games, HCI research and 2023-2026 products say about it.

Note on sources: several primary PDFs (Pousman & Stasko 2006; HAL VR diegetic study) returned HTTP 503 / bot-wall during this session. Where I could only reach a summary or a secondary citation, that is flagged. Nothing below is invented; unverified recollections are listed under Gaps.

## Key Question 1: Game UI theory — diegetic vs non-diegetic vs spatial vs meta, and what the empirical research says

### Takeaway
The industry-standard vocabulary comes from Fagerholt & Lorentzon's 2009 Chalmers thesis: a 2x2 of "exists in the fiction?" x "exists in the 3D/2D game space?" giving diegetic, meta, spatial and non-diegetic UI. The empirical record is small, mixed and mostly FPS/VR: diegetic displays reliably score higher on self-reported immersion but can cost readability; the one well-controlled 20-participant lab study found a diegetic *numeric* display actually outperformed HUD icons (26% faster reloads, 70% preference), which suggests the real variable is legibility of the encoding, not where it sits.

### Cited Findings
- Fagerholt & Lorentzon (Chalmers, 2009, "Beyond the HUD") classify UI on two axes, fiction and space: **diegetic** = in fiction and in space (characters can see it); **meta** = in fiction but on the 2D screen plane (blood splatter for health); **spatial** = in the game space but not in the fiction (objective markers, Fable's glowing trail); **non-diegetic** = neither (classic HUD). Built from a study of 20+ FPS games, interviews and playtests — [SlideShare of the DICE talk](https://www2.slideshare.net/DICEStudio/beyond-the-hud-user-interfaces-for-increased-player-immersion-in-fps-games); [Packt summary of the four types](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/mastering-ui-development/9781787125520/78296d1f-f5be-4af9-8ed6-b47ece9c8bda.xhtml)
- Anthony Stonehouse, Game Developer, 27 Feb 2014: concrete examples per class. Diegetic: Metro 2033's wrist-watch shows gas-mask filter time and visibility; Assassin's Creed eagle vision highlights patrol routes; Syndicate DART overlay. Meta: CoD MW2 blood splatter; GTA IV phone (ring is diegetic, the UI is meta); NFS Hot Pursuit speedometer. Spatial: Splinter Cell Conviction projects objective text onto walls; Fable 3 glowing guide trail; Forza 4 world-anchored typography. Non-diegetic: World of Warcraft's fully customisable HUD; Mass Effect 3's weapon/power HUD styled to match the fiction — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/user-interface-design-in-video-games)
- Dead Space's RIG: health is a glowing segmented bar physically mounted on Isaac's spine (aqua >75%, green >50%, yellow <50%, blinking red <25%); oxygen and stasis meters are also on the suit's back; ammo count is projected above the weapon; inventory and map are holographic projections that do **not** pause gameplay. Lead UI designer Dino Ignacio presented the process at GDC 2013 — [Dead Space Wiki: RIG](https://deadspace.fandom.com/wiki/Resource_Integration_Gear); [VRroom analysis](https://archive.vrroom.studio/?p=31438); [Game Developer video](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-designing-i-dead-space-i-s-immersive-user-interface)
- Far Cry 2 has no minimap; the player physically holds up a paper map and a handheld military-style GPS, even while driving; the map auto-updates as the player explores — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/far-cry-2-review); [GamesRadar Top 7 maps](https://gamesradar.com/top-7-amazing-video-game-maps-do-more-just-show-way-go/2); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Cry_2)
- Marcus Andrews (EA DICE designer, 23 Feb 2010) argued that fully in-world UI is hard to achieve, that Far Cry 2's in-world elements were partly a novelty and the game still used a HUD, that Dead Space's benefits are "fairly subjective" relative to cost, that Team Fortress 2 proves players tolerate *mixed* diegetic/spatial/HUD designs, and that WoW's customisable HUD shows players want more information to perform well. Conclusion: UI must preserve functionality first; diegetic is not automatically better — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-ui-discoveries-what-players-want)
- Peacocke, Teather, Carette & MacKenzie (IEEE GEM 2015), 20 paid regular FPS players, custom Unity game, five ammo displays (bar/number/icons on HUD vs number/icons in-game beside the weapon). The diegetic **number-in-game** display was best: 35% fewer shots fired after running dry vs the worst (icons-on-HUD), 26% faster reload, preferred by 70%. The paper's related-work review notes: Babu found no immersion difference between Metro 2033/Dead Space and BioShock/RE5; Fragoso's Battlefield 3 study found diegetic displays "vague" and that "effective feedback is more important than realism"; Llanos & Jørgensen found players liked diegetic aesthetics but valued clear communication more, yet were annoyed by overloaded HUDs; Adams' heuristics: numbers for large quantities, bars for glanceable fullness, icon rows ("small multiples") only for ~5 or fewer items — [PDF](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf); [York listing](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/ieee_gem2015.html)
- Lehmusjoki (MSc, Jyväskylä, 2017), mixed-methods A/B test of diegetic vs non-diegetic FPS UI: strong evidence that diegetic interfaces were *more difficult*; some evidence experts prefer diegetic; "insufficient evidence to make claims on the effect of user interface type on player immersion" — [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/56744)
- A 20-person inventory-UI study: immersion ranked higher for the diegetic version, but overall enjoyment ranked higher for the non-diegetic version — [Theseus](https://www.theseus.fi/handle/10024/862563)
- VR shooter study (41 players): diegetic integration improved performance but not presence/enjoyment, and the benefit applied only to novices — [HAL hal-03101667](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03101667) (abstract reached only via search snippet; page itself bot-walled)
- A second VR study (39 participants) concluded the opposite: a traditional extra-diegetic HUD was "more efficient regarding information accessibility" and therefore performance — [Taylor & Francis / IJHCI 2021](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2020.1870819)
- The Galloway (2006) origin of "diegetic/non-diegetic" for games: non-diegetic = "gamic elements inside the total gamic apparatus yet outside the pretend world of character and story"; the HUD is non-diegetic — cited in [Peacocke et al.](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf)

### Inferences
- For SemPKM, "diegetic" means the room *is* the dashboard (shelf fullness = backlog size); "spatial" means floating labels/badges anchored to objects (a "3" over the mailbox); "meta" means a vignette or tint over the whole screen (a stale-project sepia wash); "non-diegetic" means the existing GridStack dashboard. All four layers can coexist, and the TF2/WoW evidence says users accept mixed layers as long as function is preserved.
- The evidence favours a rule the Peacocke paper states almost verbatim: put *exact* values in digits (even in-world, e.g. a number painted on a crate), use fullness/bars for glanceable state, and use countable icons only up to about five. Diegetic placement did not hurt when the encoding stayed numeric; it hurt when the encoding became "vague".
- Expect a readability/immersion trade-off and design for it: novices benefit from in-world cues, experts want density. Offer a toggle (Fable/TF2 pattern) rather than forcing full diegesis.

### Gaps
- The Fagerholt & Lorentzon thesis PDF itself was not retrievable this session; the four-class definitions are from DICE's own slides and textbook summaries.
- No empirical study was found that tests diegetic *information dashboards* (lists, queues, counts) in 2D top-down games; all studies are FPS/VR status readouts (ammo, health, inventory).
- The HAL and IJHCI VR studies were only seen as abstract snippets; exact task designs not verified.

## Key Question 2: Concrete patterns from games for showing information in the world

### Takeaway
Cozy/management games already have a mature vocabulary: a persistent **collection grid** with greyed/"???" slots (Stardew, Animal Crossing Critterpedia), **physical containers** whose animation signals state (Stardew shipping bin lid opens on approach), **notice boards** with a tiny ambient "new" signal (Animal Crossing's yellow bird perched on the board), **ledgers written by the character** (Moonlighter's notebook that only records perfect-price sales), **codices that notify progress in the HUD** (Hades), **calendar-as-budget** with colour-coded days (Persona 5), **at-a-glance colonist strips plus a corner alert stack** (RimWorld), **colour-coded character beacons** (The Sims' plumbob) and **pure object-based narrative** (Unpacking's ~1,000 items). Each is a reusable mapping from data type to in-world device.

### Cited Findings
- **Stardew Valley — Collections tab:** sub-tabs Items Shipped (3 pages), Fish, Artifacts, Minerals, Cooking, Achievements, Letters, Secret Notes, Journal Scraps. Fish are recorded on catch; artifacts/minerals only on donation to the Museum, and their names and descriptions stay hidden until donated (since 1.6.9). Cooking shows known-but-uncooked recipes greyed out and unknown recipes as "???" — [Stardew Valley Wiki: Collections](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Collections)
- **Stardew Valley — Shipping Bin:** a physical box on the farm; its lid opens when the player approaches; it is the sole sale channel for some items; it can be moved/demolished since 1.5. Player criticism: no on-screen label, so new players do not know what it is — [Wiki: Shipping Box](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Shipping_Box); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/0/1738882798070742591)
- **Stardew Valley — Museum:** donations are placed on a grid of designated display spots the player arranges by hand; players complain the UI overlay can obstruct items — [Wiki: Library](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Library); layout guide (low-quality SEO source, treat with caution) [cert.tma.uz](https://cert.tma.uz/raw-ablazer-mt-002/stardew-valley-museum-layout.html)
- **Animal Crossing — Bulletin board:** a noticeboard outside Resident Services where villagers, Nook and players post messages; **a small yellow bird perches on it when there is an unread notice** (a cue used since City Folk); Nook advertises Point Specials three days ahead on it — [Nookipedia: Bulletin board / Yellow Bird](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Yellow_Bird)
- **Animal Crossing — Critterpedia:** a NookPhone app listing bugs, fish and sea creatures caught; shows whether each has been donated to the museum (small owl icon); completion unlocks exhibits and golden-tool recipes — [Nookipedia: Critterpedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Critterpedia); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-museum); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-complete-critterpedia-animal-crossing-new-horizons)
- **Animal Crossing — Museum:** donated items are exhibited physically and Blathers gives a fact per item — [Nookipedia: Museum](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Museum)
- **Moonlighter — shop notebook:** protagonist Will keeps a notebook recording sale prices; customers react with emoji faces along a four-point spectrum (delighted to grumbling); **only sales at the neutral "perfect price" are written into the notebook**, which players found inconsistent and "flawed"; critics split on whether the hi/lo pricing loop is a puzzle or busywork. Moonlighter 2 keeps the reaction signal and adds quality/display multipliers — [Nintendo Wire review](https://nintendowire.com/news/2018/11/05/review-moonlighter-on-switch/); [allthings.how price guide](https://allthings.how/moonlighter-price-guide-perfect-selling-prices-for-every-item/); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/606150/discussions/0/1697174779858273462); [Prima: Moonlighter 2 shop](https://primagames.com/?p=357379)
- **Hades — Codex and Mirror of Night:** the Codex is a book of entries (text + images) and "every time an entry has made progress you are notified in the HUD"; the Mirror of Night is a permanent-upgrade screen opened from a physical mirror in Zagreus' bedroom (red = base upgrades, yellow = alternatives); Supergiant iterated the Mirror screen across major updates — [Medium: How Hades Creates a Responsive Underworld](https://medium.com/@Nat.Rowley/how-hades-creates-a-responsive-underworld-915715a7c2a); [Fextralife: Mirror of the Night](https://hades.wiki.fextralife.com/Mirror+of+the+Night); [Supergiant patch notes](https://supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-the-nighty-night-update-patch-notes)
- **Persona 5 — calendar and social stats:** the in-game calendar is the central budget; days are colour-coded (e.g. purple = free social-stat events; story-locked days block optional activities); five Social Stats (Knowledge, Guts, Proficiency, Kindness, Charm) act as gates; Confidants are the successor to Persona 3/4's Social Links — [jeu.video confidant guide](https://jeu.video/en/guide/persona-5-royal-confidants-best-order-guide); [Christian Post tips](https://christianpost.com/news/persona-5-tips-and-tricks-quiz-guides-and-test-answers.html) (fan/guide sources; no developer analysis found)
- **RimWorld — colonist bar and alerts:** a strip at the top of the screen shows each colonist's portrait, name and status icons, grouped by settlement/caravan; left-click jumps the camera to them; alerts stack in the top-right (mental break imminent, needs rescue). A design write-up argues the whole colonist UI is built for at-a-glance visual reading rather than text — [RimWorld Wiki: User interface](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/User_interface); [Moonlit Development: RimWorld's at-a-glance UI](https://moonlitdevelopment.squarespace.com/development-blog/2017/12/25/the-designers-folder-rimworlds-at-a-glance-user-interface-elements)
- **The Sims — needs bars and plumbob:** six need bars in a panel; the plumbob (diamond over the Sim's head) summarises them: green = all high, yellow = some low, red = several critically low or strong bad moodlets; needs that require attention turn yellow/red even while the panel is closed; community mods exist because vanilla bars were "blurry" and to recolour full = light green, empty = maroon — [Sims Wiki: Plumbob](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Plumbob); [EA forum](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-general-discussion-en/where-do-i-see-my-sims-needs-bar/1585527); [ModTheSims: Clearer Need Bars](https://modthesims.info/d/650337/clearer-need-bars.html)
- **Unpacking (Witch Beam, 2021):** almost no text; the story is told entirely through ~1,000 household items the player places; the team started from existing items, added placeholders for story beats, filled with plausible objects; big beats kept simple because objects are "blunt and imprecise tools" ("duplo blocks"). GDC 2022 talk — [Game Developer GDC 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/gdc-2022-unpacking-a-narrative-through-1-000-household-items); [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/telling-story-someones-belongings-unpacking); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unpacking_(video_game))

### Inferences
- Mapping for SemPKM data types: collection (books/videos consumed) -> Critterpedia-style grid with silhouettes for unread/unseen and an "owl icon" for consumed; backlog -> shipping-bin-style container whose lid/fullness changes; notifications/recent activity -> notice board with an animated creature perched when unread; relationships/people -> Persona-style ranks or RimWorld-style portrait strip; due/overdue -> plumbob-style colour beacon over the relevant object or NPC; progress on a reading/learning path -> Hades Codex book that flashes when an entry advances.
- Moonlighter's complaint (ledger only records some events) is a warning: if the in-world ledger is lossy or lags the real data, users distrust the whole room. In-world displays must be derived live from the graph, never a separate store.
- Stardew's unlabeled shipping bin shows the discoverability cost of pure diegesis: ship a spatial hover label or signpost for every interactive object at least until the user has used it once.
- Unpacking proves that object choice alone can carry rich meaning, but the devs' "duplo blocks" caveat means encode only coarse states (empty / some / full / overflowing; fresh / dusty) in sprites and leave fine values to numbers or the overlay.

### Gaps
- No developer-authored UI rationale was found for Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing or Persona (only wikis, guides and fan analyses).
- Harvest Moon, Papers Please, A Short Hike and Dwarf Fortress were not researched in depth within the call budget; Dwarf Fortress search returned no UI-specific sources.
- No gameuidatabase.com or interfaceingame.com entry was fetched directly (sites are JS-heavy); they remain the best place to pull screenshots for the above patterns.

## Key Question 3: Virtual libraries and media shelves — skeuomorphic bookshelf UIs and research on shelf metaphors

### Takeaway
Shelf metaphors keep being rebuilt because users genuinely want shelf-style browsing (Blended Shelf, Virtual Shelf, MyShelf, and the finding that digital libraries have "no analogue of library shelves"), but the decorative wood-veneer skeuomorphism of iBooks was dropped in iOS 7 (Nov 2013) as "visual noise" that "doesn't really work like a bookshelf"; Delicious Library 3 went the other way the same year, offering 20 wood types. The lesson: keep the *shelf as information structure* (spine width, adjacency, availability, size) and drop ornament that carries no data.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's iBooks 3.2 update for iOS 7 (14 Nov 2013) "ditches the skeuomorphic look of its predecessor in favor of a simplified appearance"; the wood shelf that swivelled "like a secret revolving door" to reveal the Store was replaced by a plain grey background — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/13/11/14/apple-updates-ibooks-itunes-u-with-fresh-flatter-looks-for-ios-7)
- Critics' rationale: designer Yves Behar said the digital bookshelf "doesn't really work like a bookshelf" and called the decoration "visual noise"; others called skeuomorphism a regression; some users said iBooks was "one app where the skeuomorphic look really worked" — [AppleInsider forum: designers divided](https://forums.appleinsider.com/discussion/152504); [MacRumors thread](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/ibooks-and-itunes-u-updated-with-ios-7-redesigns.1671714/page-5)
- Delicious Library 3 (2013) was "unapologetically skeuomorphic", "taking skeuomorphism to the next level" while most were abandoning it, with 20 wood types for shelves — [TidBITS FunBITS](https://tidbits.com/2013/11/01/funbits-delicious-library-3/); [MacG test](https://www.macg.co/tests/2013/04/test-de-delicious-library-3-70392)
- **Bohemian Bookshelf** (Thudt, Hinrichs, Carpendale, CHI 2012; tech report 2011): five interlinked visualisations of a book collection designed for *serendipity*, with design goals of multiple visual access points, previews, and playful exploration — [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/7434); [Calgary InnoVis](https://innovis.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/Research/BohemianBookshelf); [CHI 2012 record](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/items/450d5475-3aa7-42b7-becd-c967277afdc0/full)
- **Blended Shelf** (Kleiner, Konstanz): 3D shelf visualisation of library holdings integrating "real-world attributes like the size and availability of books"; a comprehensive field study "confirmed the importance of shelf browsing for the users" — [KOPS Konstanz PDF](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/245c5a47-4238-40a9-aa35-3b02e2f34d8f/content); [E-LIS](https://eprints.rclis.org/22434/)
- **Virtual Shelf** (UC Berkeley iSchool 2008-09): Flex prototype of a shelf, 15-user study, aimed at making physical browsing tasks work online; the Open Library implementation lets users click a spine to open details — [Berkeley write-up](https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i247/s08/projects/virtualshelf/virtualshelf_writeup.pdf); [Open Library docs](https://www.openlibrary.org/docs/virtualshelf)
- **MyShelf** (Heinz, Mandl, Womser-Hacker, 2003): browsing by virtual library shelf metaphor, exploring a document's neighbourhood, evaluated by user tests — [E-LIS](https://eprints.rclis.org/8044/)
- McKay, Buchanan & Chang: participants in a 2007 physical-vs-digital library comparison "noted there is no digital analogue of library shelves for book seeking" — [City University PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12536/1/McKayBuchananChangCameraReady.pdf)
- Jervis & Masoodian (2013) visualise *physical* shelves for collection management, noting existing systems give limited tools for shelf-like electronic browsing — [Waikato](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/items/c5c0c073-e7da-4f49-83d1-8e3180f3fe21/full)
- 2018 DBS MSc: user perceptions of academic-catalogue "virtual shelf browse" (books as icons on a shelf) via public questionnaire — [DBS eSource](https://esource.dbs.ie/items/071689f8-cdee-4a63-99b3-c4925d635f02)

### Inferences
- The shelf survives as an *information* metaphor (adjacency = relatedness, spine thickness = length, gaps = checked-out/unread, order = sort key) and dies as a *decoration* metaphor (wood grain, parallax, swivelling store door). For SemPKM's library room: encode read/unread, format (book/ebook/video spine shapes), length and recency in the sprite; do not spend pixels on veneer.
- Pixel art partially sidesteps the 2013 anti-skeuomorphism backlash because it is read as a *style* rather than a fake-realism attempt, but the Behar critique still applies: a bookshelf that cannot be browsed by adjacency, pulled from, or seen to be full is noise.
- Bohemian Bookshelf's serendipity framing matches a PKM "library" better than a catalogue: offer several cheap in-world access points (by colour/cover, by timeline, by author shelf, by "what you were reading") rather than one grid.

### Gaps
- No academic source titled "Dusty Shelves" was found; the phrase may be informal. No study was found on Goodreads/Libib/Kindle shelf UI specifically.
- No Apple statement on *why* the iBooks shelf was dropped; rationale is from critics.
- No user study comparing a pixel-art shelf against a list for a personal media backlog.

## Key Question 4: Ambient and glanceable display research applied to a room

### Takeaway
Thirty years of ambient-display research (Weiser & Brown's Dangling String, Pousman & Stasko's 2006 taxonomy, Informative Art, History Tablecloth) gives SemPKM a ready framework: each room object should be placed on a **notification scale** (ignore -> change blind -> make aware -> interrupt -> demand attention), an **abstraction level** (direct vs symbolic), and a **transition speed** (slow drift vs sudden flash), and the field's consistent finding is that aesthetic appeal and utility must be balanced, with history/temporal gradient being rare and valuable.

### Cited Findings
- Weiser & Brown, "Designing Calm Technology" (Xerox PARC, 1995): calm technology "engages both the center and the periphery of our attention, and in fact moves back and forth between the two". Exemplar: Natalie Jeremijenko's **Dangling String**, an 8-foot plastic string driven by a motor tied to Ethernet traffic: busier network, faster whirl; hung in a hallway so it is visible and audible from many offices without being obtrusive — [calmtech.com paper](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_technology)
- Pousman & Stasko, AVI 2006, "A Taxonomy of Ambient Information Systems: Four Patterns of Design": defines ambient information systems and a taxonomy along four dimensions, **Information Capacity, Notification Level, Representational Fidelity, Aesthetic Emphasis**, and derives four design patterns — [Georgia Tech PDF](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/avi06.pdf) (503 this session); [DBLP](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/avi/PousmanS06.html); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/16969326)
- Mankoff et al.'s definition adopted by Tomitsch et al.: "Ambient displays are aesthetically pleasing displays of information which sit on the periphery of a user's attention. They generally support monitoring of non-critical information." — [Tomitsch, Kappel, Lehner, Grechenig, Pervasive 2007 workshop, PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf)
- Matthews et al.'s five notification levels, adopted by both Pousman & Stasko and Tomitsch: **ignore, change blind, make aware, interrupt, demand attention**; high notification level should use abrupt/fast transitions (flash, beep), low levels subtle/calm transitions — [Tomitsch et al. PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf)
- Tomitsch et al. (2007) analysed 51 projects and rate 19 systems on nine dimensions: abstraction level (low/medium/high), transition (slow/medium/fast), notification level, temporal gradient (most systems show only one state; few show history), representation, modality, source, privacy, dynamic of input. Example systems and encodings: **History Tablecloth** — a halo under an object grows the longer it is left on the table; **Power-Aware Cord** — glowing pulses whose flow and intensity show energy use; **Forecast Umbrella** — glow intensity = probability of rain; **Datafountain** — water-jet heights = currency rates; **Hello.Wall** — light patterns show activity in a social space; **Nimio** — objects glow when there is activity near a paired object; **Flashbag** — a USB stick that physically inflates as it fills; **Progress Bar** — tracks long-term goals to create an emotional link to passing time; **Wattson** — household power meter; **Interactive Waterfall** — watercolour ripples = activity level; **Nabaztag** — rabbit whose ears/lights signal messages — [Tomitsch et al. PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf)
- **Informative Art** (Redström, Skog, Hallnäs 2000; Holmquist & Skog GRAPHITE 2003): "combines a dynamically updated information display with the decorative role of visual art, such as posters and paintings". A Mondrian-style composition showed current weather in six cities; a Richard Long-style landscape showed 30 days of global earthquakes; a bus-departure visualisation in an abstract-art style was deployed in a public space with ~300 potential users (Skog, Ljungblad, Holmquist, INFOVIS 2003, "Between Aesthetics and Utility"). Key tension: must "both provide valuable information and present an attractive addition to the environment" — [Northumbria listing](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/informative-art-information-visualization-in-everyday-environment/); [UMBC course PDF](https://www.csee.umbc.edu/courses/graduate/CMSC691B/spring06/papers/2AmbientVis.pdf); [DBLP Redström](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/pid/56/3495.html)
- **InfoCanvas** (Miller & Stasko, CHI 2001): a hybrid ambient display with a user-designed pictorial scene whose elements (e.g. a beach scene) are bound to data — [Georgia Tech CHI01 slides](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/gvu/ii/talks/chi01.pdf)
- Amber Case formalised calm-technology principles in 2015; the Calm Tech Institute launched a certification in 2024 across six categories including attention, periphery and sound — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_technology)

### Inferences
- The room is literally an InfoCanvas: a user-arranged picture whose elements are bound to SPARQL results. SemPKM already has the binding half (SPARQL/stats blocks); the design work is a catalogue of sprite "channels" per object.
- Proposed channel table (derived from the examples above): **plant** health/wilt = days since a project was touched (History Tablecloth's "halo grows with time" inverted); **lamp** on/off/flicker = tasks due today (make-aware level); **window** = real weather/time of day (direct, low abstraction, "ignore" level, pure ambience like Weather Patterns); **candle/hourglass** = time to next event; **letters in a mailbox / bird on the notice board** = unread activity (Nabaztag/Hello.Wall pattern); **NPC** holding a scroll = a message or due item that must be acknowledged (interrupt level, because NPCs can walk toward you); **fire in the hearth** = streak or recent activity volume (Dangling String: rate -> motion).
- Respect the notification ladder: most objects should live at "ignore"/"change blind" (slow drift, e.g. dust accumulating over weeks); only due/overdue items get "make aware" (a glow, a bird); only hard deadlines get "interrupt" (NPC approaches, bubble). Nothing in the room should "demand attention" except genuine alerts.
- Temporal gradient is the under-served dimension: a room can show history cheaply (stack of past journals grows, wall calendar pages, a trophy shelf), which flat dashboards rarely do well.

### Gaps
- The exact Pousman & Stasko scale levels for Information Capacity and Representational Fidelity and the names of the four patterns could not be verified (PDF 503 twice); only the four dimension names and the Matthews notification scale are confirmed via secondary citation. From memory the patterns are Symbolic Sculptural Display, Multiple-Information Consolidators, Information Monitor Display and High-Throughput Textual Display — treat as unverified.
- No study was found evaluating ambient displays *inside a game world* (as opposed to physical rooms/public spaces).
- Nathan Shedroff's work on "experience design"/information interaction was not researched within budget.

## Key Question 5: Showing quantity and status without numbers — data physicalization precedent

### Takeaway
Data physicalization (Jansen, Dragicevic et al., CHI 2015) defines the idea precisely: an artifact "whose geometry or material properties encode data". The ambient-display corpus already supplies tested encodings (height of water jets, inflation of a USB stick, halo size, glow intensity, pulse rate) that translate directly to pixel-art sprites: stack height, pile spread, shelf fullness, dust/desaturation, glow, weather, crowd size. Game-UI heuristics add the limit: countable icons work only up to ~5; beyond that use fullness or a digit.

### Cited Findings
- Jansen, Dragicevic, Isenberg, Alexander, Karnik, Kildal, Subramanian, Hornbæk, "Opportunities and Challenges for Data Physicalization", CHI 2015, pp. 3227-3236, DOI 10.1145/2702123.2702180: "A data physicalization (or simply physicalization) is a physical artifact whose geometry or material properties encode data." Research agenda covers how to encode data physically, interaction, enabling technologies and evaluation; argues physical form "draws people in" via familiarity and tangibility — [Lancaster eprints PDF](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/72691/1/OA_2015_04_CHI_DataPhys.pdf); [Bath listing](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/opportunities-and-challenges-for-data-physicalization/)
- Physical/ambient encodings documented by Tomitsch et al.: water-jet height (Datafountain = exchange rates), physical inflation (Flashbag = stored data), halo diameter growing with elapsed time (History Tablecloth), glow intensity (Forecast Umbrella = rain probability), pulse flow/intensity (Power-Aware Cord = watts), light patterns (Hello.Wall = social activity) — [PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf)
- Dangling String: rotation speed = network packet rate — [calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology)
- Game heuristics (Adams, cited in Peacocke et al.): numeric displays for "amounts of things for which you would normally use digits in the real world" and large quantities; bars/meters "can be interpreted at a glance"; icon rows for "small-integer numeric data ... around five items or less" because players cannot take in more than five at a glance; bar + number together gives both glance and detail. Half-Life 2 used silhouette icons and yellow-to-red colour coding for low ammo (Zammitto) — [GEM 2015 PDF](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf)
- Dead Space colour-steps health: aqua/green/yellow/blinking red at 75/50/25% — [Dead Space Wiki](https://deadspace.fandom.com/wiki/Resource_Integration_Gear)
- The Sims plumbob: a single three-colour summary (green/yellow/red) of six underlying bars — [Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Plumbob)
- Animal Crossing: a yellow bird on the board = boolean "unread exists" — [Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Yellow_Bird)

### Inferences
- A practical encoding ladder for a 32-64px sprite: **boolean** -> presence/absence of a small creature or light (bird, candle); **ordinal (3-4 steps)** -> colour step or sprite variant (plumbob, Dead Space RIG; wilting plant: fresh/droopy/brown/dead); **small count (<=5)** -> countable items (letters sticking out of the mailbox, books on a desk); **large count** -> fullness of a container with 4-5 frames (shelf 0/25/50/75/100%, overflowing pile) plus a hover digit; **rate** -> animation speed (hearth flames, string spin); **elapsed time** -> accumulation (dust, cobwebs, halo, stack of unread newspapers); **ratio/progress** -> a physical meter that is plausible in-fiction (hourglass, candle length, water level in a jar).
- Dataphys' "familiar and tangible draws people in" is the argument for why a pixel shelf may out-engage a table even though it is less precise; the cost is precision, which SemPKM can recover on hover/interaction (bar + number pattern).
- Consistent colour semantics across the whole palace (one meaning for yellow, one for red) matter more than per-object cleverness; the Sims and Dead Space both use a single scheme everywhere.

### Gaps
- The dataphys.org wiki list was not fetched directly; no 2020s physicalization survey (e.g. Dragicevic, Jansen & Vande Moere's handbook chapter) was retrieved within budget.
- No study was found measuring accuracy of reading quantities from pixel-art fullness sprites.

## Key Question 6: The transition problem — in-world panel, pixel-styled overlay, or the normal web dashboard?

### Takeaway
Games and hybrid tools converge on a three-tier model: (1) glance information lives in the world; (2) a *styled* overlay (Hades' Codex book, Dead Space's non-pausing hologram, Zelda-style inventory) opens on interaction; (3) full data/edit tasks drop to a conventional panel. Gather.town is the clearest hybrid precedent: walk to an object, "press X", and an ordinary web page opens in an iframe modal inside the 2D world. Trade-offs: in-world panels maximise coherence but cap density and accessibility; pixel-styled overlays (NES.css, pixel-ui-dialog) are cheap to build on HTML and keep screen-reader/keyboard semantics; reusing the existing GridStack dashboard in a side panel costs zero new maintenance but breaks the fiction.

### Cited Findings
- Dead Space: inventory, map and objectives are holographic projections from the RIG; "opening them does not pause gameplay" — [Dead Space Wiki](https://deadspace.fandom.com/wiki/Resource_Integration_Gear); [Hudsandguis: Dead Space 2](https://www.hudsandguis.com/2012/08/22/dead-space-2-diegetic-interface-design)
- Hades: the Codex is opened from the HUD/menu and presents "neatly organised text and images"; progress notifications surface in the HUD; the Mirror of Night is opened by walking to a physical mirror in Zagreus' bedroom and yields a full-screen upgrade grid — [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@Nat.Rowley/how-hades-creates-a-responsive-underworld-915715a7c2a); [Fextralife](https://hades.wiki.fextralife.com/Mirror+of+the+Night)
- Far Cry 2's "diegetic" map is, mechanically, a menu: "hitting select whips up an adjustable-scale map and GPS-unit" rendered as the character raising a paper map — [GamingNexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/Article/2050/Far-Cry-2/); Andrews notes its appeal was partly novelty and the game still kept a HUD — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-ui-discoveries-what-players-want)
- Gather.town (2D top-down virtual office): any object can be given an "Embedded website" interaction; a prompt appears when the avatar is adjacent, pressing **x** opens the URL in an iframe inside Gather, pressing x again or the close button dismisses it; URL must be https and the site must allow iframe embedding; Google Docs/Sheets/Forms embed, Drive folders do not; a "note" interaction is the fallback for sites that block embedding — [Gather help: Embedded Websites](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15910417713940); [Gather help: Interactive objects](https://support.help.gather.town/articles/5512361772-interactive-objects)
- Rooms.xyz (Things Inc., a16z-backed, beta May 2023, 1.0 later; Google invested $1M in Jan 2025): browser voxel room builder where objects are made interactive with Lua scripts; rooms are shared by link and remixable — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/16/a16z-backed-rooms-xyz-lets-you-build-interactive-3d-rooms-and-simple-games-in-your-browser); [GamesBeat 1.0](https://gamesbeat.com/rooms-hits-1-0-launch-for-user-generated-3d-rooms)
- Spirit City: Lofi Sessions (Mooncube, Steam, 8 Apr 2024, US$11.99): the room is in-world (avatar moves between fireplace, bay-window seat, desk, bed) but the **to-do list, timer, habit tracker and journal are conventional UI panels on the right side of the screen**, not in-world objects; the Spiritdex glows when a lure combination is correct and shows a red dot when a spirit is lured; reviewer criticised that visual effects don't follow time of day — [LadiesGamers review](https://ladiesgamers.com/spirit-city-lofi-sessions-review/)
- Pixel-styled HTML overlay tooling: **NES.css** is a pure-CSS 8-bit component library (default font Press Start 2P, CDN via unpkg, components only, bring your own layout) — [GitHub mirror](https://github.com/shtakai/NES.css); **pixel-ui-dialog** builds game-style dialog boxes in plain HTML/CSS, MIT — [GitHub](https://github.com/browsermage/pixel-ui-dialog); technique notes recommend stepped borders instead of border-radius and 9-slice borders via box-shadow/border-image — [skillsmp pixel-art-css](https://skillsmp.com/skills/vihaanmotwani-sudo-agent-skills-pixel-art-css-skill-md)
- Stardew Valley's museum: a UI overlay can obstruct the physical display and there is no vanilla way to hide UI — an example of in-world display and overlay fighting for the same pixels — [layout guide (low quality)](https://cert.tma.uz/raw-ablazer-mt-002/stardew-valley-museum-layout.html)
- Empirical caution for in-world panels: Fragoso's players found diegetic displays disruptive through "lack of meaningful feedback and the relative vagueness"; HUD elements were *less* disruptive than diegetic ones — cited in [GEM 2015 PDF](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf)

### Inferences
- Recommended three tiers for SemPKM: **Tier 0 glance** (sprite states, spatial badges, hover tooltip with the exact digit). **Tier 1 "lectern" overlay**: a pixel-framed HTML modal (NES.css/9-slice border) anchored to the object, non-pausing like Dead Space, rendering a *compact* htmx fragment (top 5 due items, the shelf row, the codex page); keep it to one screen with large type. **Tier 2 full dashboard**: the existing GridStack dashboard opened in the side panel or a new tab (the Gather.town "press X -> iframe" pattern) for sorting, filtering, editing and SPARQL.
- Tier 1 should be real HTML, not canvas text: it inherits accessibility (focus order, screen readers, zoom, i18n) and is cheap to maintain with the existing htmx partials; only the frame is pixel-styled. Canvas-rendered text panels fail readability and a11y and double the maintenance surface.
- Follow Hades' split: *progress* notifications surface in-world (flash/glow on the object), *reading* happens in the overlay. Follow Dead Space: do not "pause" the room when the overlay is open; let ambient animation continue so the world still feels alive.
- Spirit City shows the market tolerates (and perhaps expects) the hybrid "cozy world + flat panels" — a useful fallback if full diegesis proves too costly.

### Gaps
- No accessibility study of pixel-art game UI or NES.css-style overlays was found.
- Zelda inventory conventions were not sourced this session (no fetch); treat as common knowledge, not a cited finding.
- No source compares maintenance cost of in-world vs overlay UI in a shipping hybrid app.

## Key Question 7: 2023-2026 tools rendering notes, tasks or dashboards as a pixel/cozy room

### Takeaway
A "productivity game" category has formed on Steam (34 titles identified in Feb 2026, 9 with 500+ reviews, 3 past $1M), led by Spirit City: Lofi Sessions, Virtual Cottage/Virtual Cottage 2, Chill Corner and Mini Cozy Room, plus web/mobile RPG task apps (Habitica, Pixel Progress, Levla) and memory-palace study tools (getmemorypalace.com, Mind Palace). Almost all keep tasks in flat panels beside a cozy room; none found renders a knowledge *graph* or dashboard as room objects, which is the open niche SemPKM would occupy.

## Key Question 8 (synthesis for the brief): a room-object vocabulary for SemPKM data types, and fallback rules

### Takeaway
Combining Q1-Q7: encode coarse state in sprites and animation, exact values in hover digits, reading in a pixel-framed HTML overlay, and editing/analysis in the existing GridStack dashboard. Keep every object at the lowest notification level that still does its job, and never let the room's display diverge from the graph.

### Cited Findings
(This section synthesises findings already cited above; key anchors repeated for traceability.)
- Fullness/bars glanceable, digits for exact large counts, <=5 countable icons — [Adams via Peacocke et al.](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/GEM2015_HUDs.pdf)
- Notification ladder ignore -> change blind -> make aware -> interrupt -> demand attention — [Tomitsch et al.](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf)
- Elapsed-time halo (History Tablecloth), rate -> motion (Dangling String), boolean -> perched bird (Animal Crossing), 3-colour beacon (plumbob) — [Tomitsch](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper09.pdf); [calmtech](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology); [Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Yellow_Bird); [Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/Plumbob)
- Collection grids with hidden/"???" entries and donation markers — [Stardew Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Collections); [Nookipedia Critterpedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Critterpedia)
- Object -> embedded web view on "press X" — [Gather help](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15910417713940)

### Inferences
- **Study room (research dashboard):** desk with stacked papers = open Tasks for the active Project (stack height 0-4 frames, digit on hover); wall pinboard = recent Notes (newest pinned top-left, up to ~6 thumbnails; a tack colour = type); lamp lit = something due today, flickering = overdue; globe/map on the wall = Concepts touched this week (pins); bookshelf behind the desk = reading queue for this project; plant on the desk wilts by days since last edit; wall calendar page = next Event; a cat sleeping on the desk = no activity for 7+ days (change-blind cue).
- **Library room (consumption collection):** one shelf per medium (books, ebooks, videos as cassette/tape sprites); spine width = length; spine saturation = unread (full colour) vs read (faded/grey, Stardew greyed-recipe pattern); a bookmark ribbon = in progress with position; dust on a shelf section = nothing added or read in 30+ days; a reading chair with an open book = "currently reading", clickable; a returns cart = items flagged to drop; a Critterpedia-style wall chart for a curriculum with silhouettes for unread.
- **People/relationships:** NPC sprites in a lounge; distance from the player's chair = recency of contact; a speech bubble = pending follow-up; a framed photo wall = relationship graph neighbourhood (edges as string between photos, Persona-rank badges for strength).
- **Timeline/events:** a corridor with doors dated along the wall, or a train-station departures board (spatial UI) listing the next 5 Events.
- **Fallback rules:** open the overlay (Tier 1) whenever the user needs to *read* more than ~5 items or any body text; open the full dashboard (Tier 2) whenever the user needs to *sort, filter, edit, or run SPARQL*; always offer a non-diegetic "list view" toggle for accessibility and for expert users (WoW/TF2 lesson); label every interactive object spatially on first encounter (Stardew shipping-bin lesson); derive every sprite state live from the graph with a visible "as of" (Moonlighter ledger lesson).

### Gaps
- All of the object mappings above are design proposals derived from the cited precedents, not tested with SemPKM users; a short Wizard-of-Oz test (static mock room vs GridStack dashboard, same questions) would close the main evidence gap identified in Q1 (no empirical work on diegetic dashboards in 2D games).
