# Sharing, Co-presence and Visiting in 2D Personal Spaces (for a shared SemPKM "memory palace")

Research notes compiled 2026-11-03. Scope: what sharing/co-presence add to a 2D top-down personal space, how visiting works in existing products and games, and what CSCW/HCI says about social presence, privacy and asynchronous visiting. Evidence is separated from inference in each section. Where a claim comes from a vendor, rival, wiki or forum, that is flagged.

A note on overall evidence quality: peer-reviewed work on Gather.town specifically is thin and mostly outside CHI/CSCW main tracks (an ACM Creativity & Cognition paper, a Cyberpsychology journal paper, an MDPI case study, a RELC education paper, several arXiv conference-organiser reports). The strongest controlled studies on spatial avatars and asynchronous presence come from adjacent settings (Mozilla Hubs at CHI 2021; a VR living-room study at IEEE VR 2022). Game precedents are documented mainly through official support pages, developer talks reported second-hand, and wikis.

## KQ1. CSCW/HCI research on Gather.town and similar spaces (2020-2025): social presence, serendipity, fatigue, inequality, privacy, why usage dropped

### Takeaway
The literature consistently finds that avatar + proximity-chat spaces raise perceived social presence and "feel of walking around" relative to Zoom, and that proximity chat lets people opt into small conversations without addressing the whole room. The same literature also records thin attendance relative to plenaries, a hard cap on how many video feeds one can see, and (in classic CSCW) an unavoidable awareness-vs-privacy/disruption tradeoff. The commercial collapse of the category in 2022 coincided with offices reopening; I found no peer-reviewed study explaining the usage drop, only business reporting.

### Cited Findings

**Social presence and the "walking around" feel**
- Latulipe & Turnbull Tillman (2022), ACM Creativity & Cognition, "Curating Interactive Art for Online Conferences: Artist, Curator and Technologist Experiences in Gather.Town": reflective practice-based study (auto-ethnography by the gallery builder plus interviews with participating artists and curators) of the C&C 2021 Art Track gallery in Gather. Per the abstract, "many artists were surprised by how much engagement the platform created, both at the gallery opening and from attendees generally" and valued the gallery's "equitable, global reach"; the paper reports "a desire for future iterations, and an exploration of the platform in a hybrid context, alongside an in-person exhibition" — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3527927.3532811); [U. Manitoba HCI lab abstract](https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/Publications/details/curating-interactive-art-for-online-conferences-artist-curator-and-technolo). A search-result excerpt of the full text states that proximity chat "was valued by artists who felt that they could focus on those visitors who wanted to talk with them about their work" — [ACM DL full-text excerpt via search](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3527927.3532811) (full text is paywalled; I could not verify the sentence in context).
- "Identifying Factor Associations Emerging from an Academic Metaverse Event for Scholars in a Postpandemic World: Social Presence and Technology Self-Efficacy in Gather.Town", Cyberpsychology, Behavior, and Social Networking (2024, published online Jan 2024). Survey of scholars at an online seminar held in Gather.Town; tested whether perceived social presence and technology self-efficacy predicted satisfaction and academic interaction — [Liebert](https://www.liebertpub.com/doi/full/10.1089/cyber.2023.0020); [PubMed 38197841](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38197841). (Both pages blocked automated fetching; I could not retrieve the effect sizes. See Gaps.)
- Comparative Case Study of Teamwork on Zoom and Gather.Town, Sustainability 15(2):1629 (MDPI, 2023): reports that Gather.Town "creates a positive team atmosphere by enhancing the social presence of peers through avatars" — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/2/1629). (Fetch blocked; finding from the search excerpt. MDPI case study, small sample; treat as supportive not decisive.)
- Zhao & McClure (2024), RELC Journal, "Gather.Town: A Gamification Tool to Promote Engagement and Establish Online Learning Communities for Language Learners" — [Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00336882221097216). (Abstract only; title indicates positive engagement/community findings in language classes.)
- A small undergraduate classroom comparison of virtual communication software found Gather a "potential alternative to Zoom" on engagement, enjoyment, social connectedness and willingness to communicate, with authors noting the small sample — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380390672_A_Comparison_of_Virtual_Communication_Software_on_Perceived_Engagement_Enjoyment_Social_Connectedness_and_Willingness_to_Communicate_in_a_Virtual_Undergraduate_Classroom).
- Cool Stars 20.5 organisers (arXiv 2021): compared with Slack and Zoom alone, "GatherTown gets closer to the feel of walking around real conference or poster halls"; the Gather space "was an overwhelming success and received only positive feedback from those who used it" — but "we never had above 150 people in there, which is well below [their] Zoom plenary attendance" — [arXiv 2105.08795](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08795).
- AbGradCon 2021 organisers (arXiv 2022): Gather "combines video conferencing with spatial positioning" and offers "capabilities that do not necessarily exist in real life, such as portals to reach different parts of the conference space quickly" — [arXiv 2202.12892](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12892).
- A coding-course report (arXiv 2022): participants "are represented as small avatars within a two dimensional visual environment displayed within their web browser"; spatial chat let students join discussions by moving their avatar so they "may feel less embarrassed about raising issues because they don't have to alert the whole classroom" — [arXiv 2209.01927](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.01927).
- Technical limit recorded by 2020 workshop organisers: in Gather "each user can see a maximum of eight other users' videos at once, other users can use audio-only" — [arXiv 2012.01191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01191).

**Closest rigorous CHI study of proxemics in a spatial avatar conference**
- Williamson, Li, Vinayagamoorthy, Shamma & Cesar (2021), CHI 2021, "Proxemics and Social Interactions in an Instrumented Virtual Reality Workshop" (DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445729). Instrumented Mozilla Hubs build logging position/orientation during a real academic workshop (keynote, breakouts, hallway networking), plus observation and semi-structured interviews. Main finding: smaller rooms produced more cohesive groups, while larger rooms made small-group formation harder but gave people more flexible personal space — [arXiv 2101.05300](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.05300); [Glasgow eprint PDF](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/234460/1/234460.pdf); [dataset repo](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fayman%2Fhubs-research-acm-chi-2021).

**Serendipity**
- "Report on the Future of Conferences" (arXiv 2023) argues that most virtual conferences as designed "have limited support for one-on-one serendipitous meetings", pointing to missing presence cues and attendees who treat themselves as viewers — [arXiv 2301.03544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.03544).
- CSCW 2022 ran pre-recorded talks and the reception in a shared Gather space and also used Who2chat pairing and "Lab Speed Dating" to manufacture serendipity — [CSCW 2022 social events](https://cscw.acm.org/2022/social-events/); [CSCW blog](https://acm-cscw.medium.com/experiencing-virtual-cscw2022-conference-c1caa3c2fc0b).
- A Discord-based ICER 2020 trip report (blog, not peer-reviewed) found attendees "really appreciated how well it supported agency in movement" — [Ko, Medium](https://medium.com/bits-and-behavior/acm-icer-2020-trip-report-virtual-serendipity-6134c8ddb9d8).

**Fatigue and surveillance (video-meeting literature, not Gather-specific)**
- Field experiment with BroadPath (HBR 2021): requiring cameras on increased fatigue, especially for women and newer employees — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2021/10/research-cameras-on-or-off); summarised also by [Walton College](https://walton.uark.edu/insights/posts/zoom-fatigue-may-be-here-to-stay.php) ("self-presentation and its fatigue-related costs are amplified in a virtual meeting where the camera is turned on").
- Ethnographic/auto-ethnographic study of remote meetings (EPIC 2022, "Beyond Zoom Fatigue") notes "obvious hints at discomfort with surveillance, including potential loss of control over who gets to view one's reactions" — [EPIC](https://www.epicpeople.org/beyond-zoom-fatigue/).

**Classic CSCW on the awareness/privacy tradeoff (directly applicable to "see who is in my palace")**
- Hudson & Smith (1996), CSCW '96, "Techniques for Addressing Fundamental Privacy and Disruption Tradeoffs in Awareness Support Systems": frames a dual tradeoff — more awareness information means less privacy for the observed and more disruption for the observer — and proposes abstraction techniques — [PDF](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Hudson-CSCW96-p248-hudson.pdf); [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/cscw/HudsonS96). Röcker & Magerkurth (2007) describe the tradeoff as "widely accepted" and dispute that it is inevitable — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-73279-2_31).
- Boyle, Edwards & Greenberg (2000), CSCW 2000, "The Effects of Filtered Video on Awareness and Privacy": blur (and to a lesser extent pixelation) filters have a level "suitable for providing awareness information while safeguarding privacy" — [U. Calgary PRISM](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/items/329fd90e-197e-47b0-8087-cb2e73b15465/full). Boyle & Greenberg (2005), TOCHI, "The Language of Privacy" — a vocabulary (solitude, confidentiality, autonomy) for media-space privacy — [PDF](https://grouplab.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/grouplab/uploads/Publications/Publications/2005-LanguagePrivacy.TOCHI.pdf).

**Why usage dropped (business reporting, not research)**
- Forbes (reposted): Gather cut about a third of staff in June 2022, roughly two years after founding, after a $50M round (Sequoia, Index) at a $700M valuation in Nov 2021 — [repost of Forbes](https://kuangkunang.blogspot.com/2022/07/virtual-office-startup-gather-laid-off.html).
- An AI-generated Sacra summary (low reliability) says the category "collapsed with broad office re-openings in 2022", with Teamflow shutting down and Gather and Kumospace founding teams leaving — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/f1c1c17b-9051-46ec-aa14-6cd94e3aec7d/).
- Rival WorkAdventure's marketing page claims Gather ended its free plan and doubled prices (commercial interest; unverified) — [WorkAdventure](https://workadventu.re/gather-alternative/). Gather's own blog references a new CEO and remaining "an independent, non-venture-backed business" (date unverified) — [Gather blog](https://gather.town/blog); [createwith summary](https://createwith.com/tool/gather-town/updates/gather-town-reaffirms-independent-focus-and-announces-ai-team-transition).

### Inferences
- Across sources, the two durable benefits of a 2D avatar space are (a) a legible sense of who is here and what they are near, and (b) opt-in small-group talk by walking up, which lowers the bar for shy participants. Both are presence/awareness features, not video features, so they survive in a no-voice design.
- Attendance figures (150 in Gather vs much larger Zoom plenaries) suggest spatial spaces work for small, intentional groups, not for broadcast. A palace visit is inherently small-group, so this is a fit, not a problem.
- The Williamson room-size finding implies rooms in a palace should be sized to the number of people expected; a huge empty gallery will feel dead.
- Hudson & Smith's tradeoff predicts that "show me who is in my palace right now" will be valued by the owner and resented by visitors unless abstracted (e.g., counts, blurred/ghost avatars, delayed presence) — this is the same mechanism Boyle & Greenberg validated with blur filters.
- The commercial collapse tracks the end of forced remote work, not evidence that spatial presence stopped being valued; but it does imply a palace should not depend on people "living" in it all day.

### Gaps
- I found no CHI/CSCW main-track empirical paper on Gather.town at an academic conference, no "virtual office ethnography" of Gather/Teamflow, and no study quantifying participation inequality in Gather. The closest controlled work is in Mozilla Hubs (VR).
- Effect sizes from the Cyberpsychology 2024 Gather paper and the MDPI 2023 study could not be retrieved (403/cookie walls).
- No peer-reviewed explanation of the post-2022 usage decline; only business press and rival marketing.

## KQ2. Game precedents for visiting personal spaces: Animal Crossing, Stardew, Minecraft, Second Life, VRChat, Roblox, Sims

### Takeaway
Games have converged on a small set of visiting models: live hosted visits with tiered trust (Animal Crossing's friends vs Best Friends), offline snapshot visits (dream islands), downloadable/forkable builds (Sims Gallery, Minecraft world downloads), permanent curated public exhibitions (Info Island libraries, the Uncensored Library) and instance-based access tiers (VRChat). Social behaviours that recur: tours, gift-giving, trading, decorating for guests, and competition/comparison of collections. Hosts universally retain the ability to end or withdraw access, and destructive permissions are gated separately from entry.

### Cited Findings

**Animal Crossing: New Horizons — live visiting**
- Up to eight players on one island at once; host picks "I want visitors", visitor "I wanna fly!"; online needs Nintendo Switch Online. Visitors can trade items/fossils/DIY recipes, give gifts, talk to residents, shop at the host's stores (different stock/colours), join hosted events (birthday parties, fashion shows, campfires, treasure hunts, mazes, timed bug/fish contests), and team up for Fishing Tourney/Bug Off. Etiquette per Nintendo: "ask permission before cutting trees or collecting someone's resources" and "don't trample the flowers" — [Nintendo UK, 2020](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2020/May/Top-tips-for-playing-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons-with-friends-1783708.html).
- Tiered trust: regular visitors cannot use axe or shovel; only "Best Friends" (added via the NookPhone app) can use tools on your island, see when you're online, message you, and send presents — [Nintendo UK](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2020/May/Top-tips-for-playing-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons-with-friends-1783708.html); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/your-friends-cant-destroy-your-animal-crossing-new-horizons-island). Press coverage framed the flip side: "Your 'Best Friend' can destroy your island" — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/your-best-mate-can-destroy-your-island-animal-crossing-new-horizons); Nintendo Life published a safety/scams guide around friends vs best friends — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/animal-crossing-new-horizons-scams-friends-best-friends-and-how-to-keep-yourself-safe-online).
- A 2025 Public Humanities article, "Designing the Virtual Museum with Animal Crossing: New Horizons", draws lessons for virtual museum design including building social interaction into the experience and describes friends visiting each other's museums to compare collections — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-humanities/article/designing-the-virtual-museum-with-animal-crossing-new-horizons/93606C8166446F088E4B7528694E773B).

**Animal Crossing — Dream islands (offline snapshot visiting)**
- Added in v1.4.0 (July 2020); v1.6.0 added a "Surprise me" random-visit option (community wiki) — [Fandom](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/Dreaming).
- Nintendo support: you can visit others' islands "even while they are not playing"; "you cannot make changes to the island, since visits happen only in dreams"; a host can remove the island from the dream library, after which the Dream Address stops working; requires NSO, 5+ residents, a house and a bed, gates closed and playing solo — [Nintendo support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-(animal-crossing:-new-horizons)).
- Host flow: sleep, "I'd Like to Share a Dream"; Luna "warns you about public data"; the current island state is uploaded and assigned a 12-digit Dream Address; host can update the snapshot once per day; visitors' inventory is removed on entry and restored on waking; nothing visitors do is saved; visitors can bring back custom designs on display; the address is shown on passport and island map by default but can be made private or deleted — [How-To Geek, Aug 2020](https://www.howtogeek.com/683468/how-to-visit-someones-island-in-dreams-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons/). Dream Addresses "are usually shared by friends or found on sites like Reddit and Twitter" — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/animal-crossing-new-horizons-dreaming-guide-dream/1100-6480431/).

**Stardew Valley — hosted co-op farms**
- The save lives on the host's system; "if the host is offline or the farm isn't open, farmhands can't access the world or their characters." Each joiner needs a cabin (up to 3, or 7 on PC/Switch 2); PC allows up to 8 players. Money can be shared or split; each player keeps own skills, relationships and mailbox; Museum collection is global. Host-only: upgrade farmhouse, demolish cabins, pause, kick/ban (PC only), change building-move permissions. No way to visit a farm without the host hosting — [Stardew Valley Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer).

**Minecraft — a curated public exhibition in a game world**
- Reporters Without Borders and BlockWorks released "The Uncensored Library" on 12 March 2020; ~300 books across twelve wings; accessed via server (visit.uncensoredlibrary.com) or local download; articles are readable by everyone on the server "but their content cannot be changed"; later recognised with a Peabody Award — [RSF](https://rsf.org/en/news/rsf-opens-uncensored-library-digital-home-press-freedom-within-global-computer-game); [minecraft.net](https://minecraft.net/article/uncensored-library).

**Second Life — libraries and exhibitions**
- Info Island was created in 2007 by the Alliance Library System as "a virtual archipelago of libraries"; libraries mounted digital exhibitions (e.g., Van Gogh reproductions), hosted events, answered reference questions — [encyclopedia.pub](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/31351); [ALA Library Technology Reports](https://journals.ala.org/index.php/ltr/article/download/4324/4980); [PolyU 2009 presentation](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/1319/3/GovernmentForum_3Aug09.pdf). Sources are 2006-2010; current activity unknown.

**VRChat — instance access tiers (community wiki, not official docs)**
- Instance types: Public, Friends+, Friends, Invite+, Invite, Group Public, Group+, Group. Friends+ admits friends of friends; Friends excludes them; Invite+ lets people request to join unless someone inside has "Join Me" status; Group restricts to group members or specific roles. The instance owner holds moderation powers in every type except Public; "instances of Public type do not have an instance owner leaving the only user with comparable powers being the world author"; instance settings cannot be set by the creator for Public/Group Public — [VRChat Wiki: Instances](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Instances). (Official docs page returned 404; wiki tables were noted as not fully consistent.)

**The Sims 4 — Gallery (forkable builds, not visiting)**
- Players upload and download Households, Rooms and Lots; a downloaded Lot must be the same size or smaller than the target lot; content may be missing if the downloader lacks the packs used — [EA Help](https://help.ea.com/en/help/the-sims/the-sims-4/the-sims-4-gallery/).

**Journey — anonymous companions (synchronous but identity-free)**
- Lead designer Nick Clark: "reducing griefing was central to our vision for how Journey could stand apart"; during internal playtests, blocking the other player from a key ledge "became a badge of griefing honor" — [Game Developer, "Cutting Corners: Networking Design in Journey"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cutting-corners-networking-design-in-journey). Jenova Chen: in most games "everybody has names and IDs. And most peoples names are very aggressive"; names leak gender/age and "can ruin the experience"; visible gear "created visible, often times impenetrable hierarchies" — [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/). GDC 2013 "Designing Journey" — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017700/Designing). Outcome: players posted forum messages to unknown companions apologising for disconnects or thanking them — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/journey-producer-reveals-how-gamer-reactions-influenced-the-game/).

### Inferences
- Two separate axes recur in every precedent: *who may enter* (public / friends-of-friends / friends / invite / group) and *what they may change* (nothing / leave things / use tools / co-build). Animal Crossing's "friends can enter, only Best Friends can dig" is the cleanest model and maps directly onto a palace: members can walk and read; a smaller trusted set can place items or edit.
- The dream-island pattern (upload a snapshot, visitors wander offline, nothing persists, owner can revoke the address) is the lowest-risk and lowest-infrastructure form of sharing and is what people actually use to "show off" a finished space. It also removes the need for the host to be online, which the Stardew model shows is the main friction of hosted visits.
- Nintendo's design treats the snapshot as *public data* and says so explicitly at upload time; a palace share flow should do the same (and in SemPKM's case, say which named graph(s) the snapshot exposes).
- Exhibition precedents (Uncensored Library, Info Island) are read-only by design and justify a "gallery" room type that is published, curated and immutable to visitors.
- The Sims Gallery suggests a third mode beyond visit and co-build: export/fork a room or palace layout as a template. In SemPKM this could be a shareable room layout decoupled from the private content.

### Gaps
- No reliable sources found on Roblox "showcase" games as a visiting pattern, on VRChat official access docs (404), on Happy Home Paradise network sharing, or on guestbook/visitor-count features in Animal Crossing dreams (How-To Geek and Nintendo pages do not mention any).
- Research on emergent behaviours (tours, gifts) is mostly journalism and official tips pages; the only peer-reviewed treatment found is the 2025 Public Humanities museum article and a ResearchGate paper ("Places, people, practices, and play: ACNH here and there") whose methods I could not confirm.

## KQ3. Asynchronous presence patterns: traces, ghosts, messages, footprints, shared structures

### Takeaway
Controlled evidence (Hirsch et al. 2022, N=26) shows that implicit traces of prior use in a shared virtual room significantly increase asynchronous social presence and make the space feel "authentic", at the cost of some users finding it untidy. Game precedents (Souls messages/bloodstains, Death Stranding structures/likes, Journey's nameless companions) show that anonymous, template-constrained, non-destructive traces produce camaraderie while limiting griefing. Classic HCI (Footprints, 1999) shows interaction history reduces navigation effort.

### Cited Findings

**Controlled studies**
- Hirsch, Haller, Butz & George (2022), IEEE VR 2022 workshop paper, "'What a Mess!': Traces of Use to Increase Asynchronous Social Presence in Shared Virtual Environments". Within-subject study, N=26 (ages 15-63, M=41), a virtual living room with vs without traces of use (13 objects; trace types after Baxter et al.: object characteristics, state, setting, context). Results: social presence significantly higher with traces (Mann-Whitney U=502, p=.003; M=3.23 vs M=-1.46); participants looked at objects more often (W=132, p=.015) and longer (W=140.5, p=.0044), and stayed ~30 s longer (321 s vs 291 s). Twelve participants liked the traced room as "homely", "inviting", "vivid", "authentic"; six preferred the untraced room because "they felt more comfortable in tidy environments and could forget real-world duties". Authors note "in the virtual environment, spaces can always be reset by the system or users" and that "work on asynchronous interactions and social presence is still limited" — [PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/hirsch_ieeeVR22/hirsch_ieeeVR22.pdf).
- Hirsch, George & Butz (2022), ISMAR 2022, "Traces in Virtual Environments: A Framework and Exploration": framework for designing social VEs around traces of use; reports traces increase social presence but their meaning "may be ambiguous"; cites game precedents where players leave marks to coordinate asynchronously and No Man's Sky messages — [PDF](https://www.mmi.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/hirsch2022ismar/hirsch2022ismar.pdf).
- Wexelblat & Maes (1999), CHI '99, "Footprints: History-Rich Tools for Information Foraging": interaction-history visualisations (maps, paths, annotations) for web navigation; in a controlled browse task users "were able to get the same amount of work done with significantly less effort"; thesis framing: "information from past users can help direct present problem-solvers" — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/29146); [bibliographic record](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-1999-WexelblatM.html).

**Souls games (messages, bloodstains, ghosts)**
- Nicholls & Cook (2022), Foundations of Digital Games, "The Dark Souls of Archaeology: Recording Elden Ring": "Soulsborne games are an example of asynchronous multiplayer, sometimes referred to as mingleplayer", in which "players feel the impact and presence of other players without direct live interaction between them"; players "leave messages on the ground using fixed templates and a limited vocabulary"; bloodstains replay "the last moments of their life" as a red ghost; "Ghosts are non-interactive and do not affect the current player's world"; messages "warn about dangers, guide to secrets, make jokes" and bloodstains "create an anonymous camaraderie among players" — [arXiv 2204.10949](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.10949).
- "Why Do We Suffer for Fun? Ordeal Pleasure in Souls-like Games" (arXiv 2026): the message system "transforms individual struggle into visible collective experience, as players encounter evidence of others' presence and solidarity without synchronous multiplayer" — [arXiv 2603.26677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26677).
- Soapstone messages use a template vocabulary; a message "will only be sent to other players while the author of the message is online and connected" (community wiki) — [Fextralife](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Orange+Guidance+Soapstone). Fragility: when Demon's Souls servers shut down, its messages and bloodstains were lost; a data miner charted them — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/someones-turning-player-death-data-in-dark-souls-into-m-1823746464).
- "Asynchronous Interactions Between Players and Game World" (Digicom 2020, Springer) treats Death Stranding as the exemplar of "strand games" built on asynchronous interaction — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-61671-7_14) (paywalled; abstract only).

**Death Stranding (shared structures, likes)**
- Kojima Productions calls the system the "Social Strand System"; roads, bridges and safe houses "can be built across the Social Strand System, and players may contribute to the construction"; "well-trodden paths will smooth over in time"; likes are given to the builder of a structure and earned for structures, signs, contributing resources, or delivering lost cargo (fan wiki) — [Death Stranding wiki](https://deathstranding.fandom.com/wiki/Social_Strand_System); developer-interview coverage — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/kojima-details-the-subtly-social-gameplay-of-i-death-stranding-i-); Director's Cut expanded the system with more coordination tools — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/125784/a-big-goal-of-death-stranding-directors-cut-was-improving-the-asynchronous-multiplayer).

**Journey** — see KQ2: anonymity chosen explicitly to prevent griefing and status hierarchies; companions later thanked on forums — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cutting-corners-networking-design-in-journey); [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/).

### Inferences
- The Hirsch result is the single most useful datapoint for a palace: *visible wear* (a book left open, a path worn in, a note on a desk) measurably increases the feeling that others have been here, without any live infrastructure. The 12-vs-6 split also says traces must be optional or confinable (e.g., a "tidy" view, or traces only in public rooms).
- Souls-style constraints (template phrases, non-interactive ghosts, no direct chat) are a proven way to allow visitor marks while bounding abuse. Comments-on-objects already exist in SemPKM; placing them *in the room* as pick-up-able notes is the natural equivalent.
- Death Stranding's "likes" and "well-trodden paths" suggest two cheap, meaningful signals: appreciation on a room/object, and path heatmaps showing which exhibits visitors actually walk to. Footprints (1999) is the HCI lineage for the same idea.
- Server-shutdown loss of Souls messages argues for storing visitor traces as ordinary RDF data in a named graph, so they survive and can federate like everything else in SemPKM.

### Gaps
- I found no study that measures asynchronous presence specifically in 2D top-down spaces; the controlled evidence is VR.
- No primary Miyazaki or Kojima design document explaining the reasoning behind messages/likes; all developer rationale is via journalism.
- No research found on "last seen here" or guestbook mechanics in virtual spaces specifically.

## KQ4. Knowledge-sharing precedents: digital gardens, Obsidian Publish, learning in public, Roam, Are.na; what makes a shared knowledge space worth visiting

### Takeaway
The digital-garden literature (Appleton 2020, Caufield 2015) argues that a public personal knowledge space is worth visiting because it is topological rather than chronological, shows work in progress with explicit status, and presents a "higher-fidelity" picture of the author. Tooling (Obsidian Publish, Roam public graphs, Are.na channels) converges on selective publishing plus optional collaborators. A critique (Joel Chan) flags that excerpted knowledge loses value without its surrounding connections and that sharing thoughts carries harm risks.

### Cited Findings
- Appleton, "A Brief History & Ethos of the Digital Garden" (2020): traces the term to Bernstein's "Hypertext Gardens" (1998) ("Unplanned hypertext sprawl is wilderness: complex and interesting, but uninviting") and Caufield's "The Garden and the Stream" (2015) ("The Garden is the web as topology"). Six patterns: Topography over Timelines; Continuous Growth; Imperfection & Learning in Public; Playful, Personal and Experimental; Intercropping & Content Diversity; Independent Ownership. On why gardens are worth visiting: "Many entry points but no prescribed pathways"; readers see the author "doing The Work"; gardens offer "higher-fidelity" self-presentation "with quirks, contradictions, and complexity". On curation: "Publishing imperfect and early ideas requires that we make the status of our notes clear to readers" (Seedling/Budding/Evergreen) — [maggieappleton.com](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history).
- swyx, "Learn in Public": advocates producing "learning exhaust" (notes, tutorials, talks) rather than learning in private and lurking; framed as the fastest way to learn and to build a network, not altruism — [Jim Nielsen's notes](https://notes.jim-nielsen.com/n/2019-05-28-1225/); follow-up "Learning Gears" — [swyx.io](https://swyx.io/learning-gears.md).
- Obsidian Publish: "Select the notes you want to share with the world, press Publish"; custom domain; permalinks; "Share a Publish site with collaborators"; multiple sites — [Obsidian help](https://obsidian.md/help/publish). (Password protection and graph view are known Publish features but were not on the fetched page; treat as unverified here.)
- Roam Research: Pro plan lists "Unlimited Private or Public Roam Graphs" and "Unlimited Collaborators" (third-party listing) — [TechJockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/roam). Joel Chan on multiplayer graphs: "how do we protect unauthorized or unwanted access to ideas and thoughts that might lead to harm for the knowledge sharer?" and shared entry points lose value without their surrounding connections — [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1309521782806847490). A "learning in public" workflow built on Roam gave a community "visibility into what every user is working on" — [DEV](https://dev.to/odyslam/a-system-for-learning-in-public-with-roam-research-62h).
- Are.na: links, images, text and files collected into "channels" that can be public or private; "Unlike a 'Like' or a simple 'Share,' the platform demands a degree of cognitive effort" and "a similar process of self-editing and mutual generosity" to filing — [Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/68557/A-Digital-Platform-Advocating-the-Importance-of-Creative-Research); channels can be set Closed and collaborators invited (course material) — [typography course](https://typography-interaction-2324.github.io/project/4).
- The Uncensored Library (KQ2) is a curated, read-only knowledge exhibition in a game world: ~300 articles in twelve themed wings, readable but immutable — [RSF](https://rsf.org/en/news/rsf-opens-uncensored-library-digital-home-press-freedom-within-global-computer-game).

### Inferences
- A memory palace is literally "the web as topology": rooms and adjacency are the organising principle, which is exactly what garden theory says makes a shared knowledge space navigable and worth wandering. Appleton's "many entry points, no prescribed pathways" fits a free-roam visit; a guided "tour" is the complementary stream-like mode.
- Curation differs from a raw dump by (a) status signals on each exhibit (seedling/evergreen; in RDF terms an explicit maturity/visibility predicate), (b) selective publication (Obsidian Publish's per-note choice; Are.na's per-channel public/closed), and (c) keeping enough context around each item that it still makes sense (Chan's critique). A shared palace should publish rooms, not isolated objects, so the surrounding connections travel with the exhibit.
- The strongest value proposition in the sources is relational: visitors come to see *how a person thinks*, not to retrieve facts. That favours showing the owner's arrangement and annotations over a generic graph view.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found on what makes a digital garden get visited or on visitor behaviour in public PKM spaces; the evidence is essayistic.
- Obsidian Publish password protection / graph view not verified from the fetched page; Roam's current multiplayer status not verified from a primary source.

## KQ5. Permission and privacy models: per-room visibility, visitor capabilities, identity/anonymity, linking to a shared graph, moderation

### Takeaway
Existing systems separate *entry* from *capability* and layer identity on top: Animal Crossing (friends enter, Best Friends alter), VRChat (eight access tiers with owner/author moderation), Gather (members bypass password/guest-list restrictions; guests are gated), Stardew (host-only destructive actions, kick/ban), Dream islands (revocable snapshot with explicit "public data" warning). Research adds that awareness features trade against privacy and disruption (Hudson & Smith) and that abstraction (blur/counts) preserves awareness while protecting privacy (Boyle & Greenberg), and that anonymity reduces griefing but also removes accountability (Journey).

### Cited Findings
- Entry tiers: VRChat Public / Friends+ / Friends / Invite+ / Invite / Group Public / Group+ / Group; instance owner moderates in all but Public; world author has powers in most types — [VRChat Wiki](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Instances).
- Gather space access: Space Password (members "will not need to enter the password"); email-domain restriction (paid); guest check-in (office spaces); guest list (conference spaces; "only users whose email is on the list can enter", overrides the password, tied to the space not an account, and "you must add your own email, even as a space Admin/Owner"); "If someone is a Member of a space, they can enter regardless of these settings"; changing the password sends people inside back to spawn tiles — [Gather help](https://support.gather.town/hc/en-us/articles/15975751960340).
- Capability tiers: ACNH visitors cannot use axe/shovel; Best Friends can — [Nintendo UK](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2020/May/Top-tips-for-playing-Animal-Crossing-New-Horizons-with-friends-1783708.html). Stardew host-only actions (farmhouse upgrade, demolish cabin, pause, kick/ban, building-move permission) — [Stardew Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Multiplayer).
- Revocation and disclosure: dream islands can be removed from the library (address stops working); Luna "warns you about public data" before upload; address visible on passport by default but can be made private — [Nintendo support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-(animal-crossing:-new-horizons)); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/683468/how-to-visit-someones-island-in-dreams-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons/).
- Read-only exhibitions: Uncensored Library articles "cannot be changed" by visitors — [RSF](https://rsf.org/en/news/rsf-opens-uncensored-library-digital-home-press-freedom-within-global-computer-game).
- Anonymity: Journey removed names, IDs, chat to prevent aggression and hierarchy — [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/jenova-chen-reveals-secrets-of-journey-design/); Souls messages are template-only and ghosts cannot affect your world — [arXiv 2204.10949](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.10949).
- Awareness vs privacy: Hudson & Smith (1996) dual tradeoff — [PDF](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Hudson-CSCW96-p248-hudson.pdf); Boyle et al. (2000) blur preserves awareness while safeguarding privacy — [PRISM](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/items/329fd90e-197e-47b0-8087-cb2e73b15465/full); Boyle & Greenberg (2005) privacy vocabulary: solitude, confidentiality, autonomy — [TOCHI PDF](https://grouplab.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/grouplab/uploads/Publications/Publications/2005-LanguagePrivacy.TOCHI.pdf).
- Knowledge-sharing harm: Chan on unauthorised access to "ideas and thoughts that might lead to harm for the knowledge sharer" — [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1309521782806847490). Obsidian Publish: per-note selection; collaborators can be added to a site — [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/help/publish).
- Video-meeting surveillance discomfort: "potential loss of control over who gets to view one's reactions" — [EPIC](https://www.epicpeople.org/beyond-zoom-fatigue/).

### Inferences (design options, grounded in the above)
- Model the palace share as *rooms bound to named graphs*: a room's visibility is the visibility of the graph(s) it exhibits. SemPKM already shares named graphs via RDF Patch and LDN invitations, so "publish this gallery to the shared graph G" and "this study stays in my private graph" reuse existing machinery and make the dream-island "this is public data" warning precise (list the graphs being exposed).
- Separate three permission ladders, following ACNH/VRChat/Gather: entry (owner only / instance members / invited WebIDs / federation partners / anyone with link), capability (walk+read / leave a note or like / place an item / co-edit room layout / co-edit content), and identity (named WebID / pseudonymous / anonymous ghost). Defaults from precedent: members walk+read; invited trusted users leave notes; co-edit is opt-in per room.
- Live presence for the owner ("who is in my palace now") should follow Boyle & Greenberg's abstraction principle: show counts and ghost avatars by default, names only for mutually-connected WebIDs; and give visitors a "solitude" mode (invisible browsing) since visitors to a knowledge space are reading, which is sensitive.
- Moderation precedents: host can always close the gates / end the visit (ACNH, Stardew), kick/ban (Stardew PC, VRChat owner), and revoke the snapshot (dreams). Since SemPKM has comments already, visitor notes should flow through the same moderation (owner can hide/delete) and be stored as RDF so they federate.

### Gaps
- No research found that evaluates per-room visibility in a personal virtual space; the per-room model is inferred from product practice (VRChat instances, Gather areas) rather than studied.
- Gather's full role matrix (Owner/Admin/Builder/Moderator/Member) was referenced but not retrieved ("User Roles & Permissions" article not fetched).
- No sources on how WebID/Solid-style identity has been used for virtual-space access control.

## KQ6. Real-time collaboration technology constraints for a self-hosted htmx + vanilla JS app

### Takeaway
Presence-only (avatar positions, no voice) needs only a WebSocket (or even SSE + POST) relay broadcasting small position deltas at ~10-20 Hz per room; the heavy parts of WorkAdventure/Gather (LiveKit SFU, coturn TURN, Jitsi, Matrix) exist solely for audio/video and chat. Colyseus (Node) is the reference authoritative room server with a 50 ms default patch rate. Asynchronous visiting (snapshot + traces stored as data) needs no real-time infrastructure at all, which is why Nintendo's dream islands work with the host offline.

### Cited Findings
- WorkAdventure self-hosting (official install doc): the platform runs as "a set of Docker containers"; components listed: WorkAdventure core (required), LiveKit ("manages audio/video when a bubble has more than 4 users"; required for group A/V), Coturn (relays WebRTC for users whose networks block P2P; "without Coturn, about 15% of users may fail to establish audio/video"), Jitsi (alternative for large rooms, being superseded), OIDC provider (optional SSO), Synapse/Matrix (optional; without it "users can't message each other outside a shared bubble or while offline, and chats aren't saved"). Typical production: one server for WorkAdventure plus two more (LiveKit, Coturn) each with its own public IP/DNS; expects "strong Docker/container skills" and about one update per month — [install.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/workadventure/workadventure/develop/docs/others/self-hosting/install.md).
- WorkAdventure media path: conversation bubbles use full-mesh P2P WebRTC up to 4 users, then "seamlessly upgrade the video to Livekit" (an SFU) on the 5th; LiveKit chosen because it is "an open-source low-level SFU" with no imposed UI; Jitsi "imposes its interface" — [WorkAdventure blog](https://workadventu.re/article-en/improving-the-virtual-conversation-and-communication-experience-with-workadventure/); [1.27 release notes](https://workadventu.re/release/workadventure-1-27-0-the-road-to-workadventure-2/). Network requirements: HTTPS 443 for core; UDP 443 and 10000-20000 for coturn; Cloudflare TURN/STUN fallbacks; LiveKit Cloud "when streaming a huge conference or when our servers are overloaded" — [network doc](https://docs.workadventu.re/admin/preparing-your-network).
- WorkAdventure internals (weak evidence, from a mirrored repo): top-level `pusher` and `back` services; package.json includes `google-protobuf` and `grpc`; pusher reads `GRPC_PORT` defaulting to 50051; front selects a pusher URL from map properties — [kraut.space mirror commit](https://git.kraut.space/Kraut.World/server/commit/cdd61bdb2c7dc48965d739e6b8e08d05eba2e23c). I could not find official documentation of the pusher/back/zone position-sync protocol.
- Colyseus (Node.js authoritative room server): rooms with `onCreate/onJoin/onLeave/onDispose`; state defined with Schema; "Only the latest mutation of each property is queued and sent to clients during the patchRate interval"; WebSocket default transport (alternatives uWebSockets.js, WebTransport, Bun); client SDKs for JS/TS, Unity, Godot, Defold etc.; 0.18 adds client prediction/rollback/interpolation; horizontal scaling across processes — [docs](https://docs.colyseus.io/); [state docs](https://docs.colyseus.io/state). Source defaults: `const DEFAULT_PATCH_RATE = 1000 / 20; // 20fps (50ms)`; `#_maxClients = Infinity`; simulation timestep default ~16.67 ms; `allowReconnection(client, seconds | "manual")` — [Room.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/colyseus/colyseus/master/packages/core/src/Room.ts).
- Gather behaviour as seen by users: "If you move your avatar sufficiently close to other avatars, you'll be connected to each of those users (with audio and video...); but the connection will be lost as soon as you walk away"; it "may get heavy on your computer's processing power and internet bandwidth when there are many people in the same local space" — [EBI help sheet](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/materials/single-cell-rna-seq-analysis-using-python-handbook-2025/help-sheets/gather-town); max eight simultaneous videos — [arXiv 2012.01191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01191). No Gather engineering blog describing its sync architecture was found.
- Asynchronous visiting needs no live host: dream islands are uploaded snapshots visited "even while they are not playing", updated at most once per day, with visitor actions discarded — [Nintendo support](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/50094/~/how-to-visit-another-players-dream-island-(animal-crossing:-new-horizons)); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/683468/how-to-visit-someones-island-in-dreams-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons/). Souls messages are only visible while the author is online (per community wiki) — a design SemPKM need not copy — [Fextralife](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/Orange+Guidance+Soapstone).

### Inferences
- Everything expensive in WorkAdventure (LiveKit, coturn, Jitsi, Synapse, extra public IPs) is for voice/video/chat. A presence-only palace needs: one WebSocket endpoint per palace instance (or one per room, as Colyseus does), a server-held map of `{webid → x, y, facing, room}`, and a broadcast of changed positions every 50-100 ms. That is a few hundred lines in the existing Python backend (e.g. an ASGI WebSocket route) and no new containers. Clients can interpolate between updates (Colyseus's 20 Hz default is a reasonable target; 10 Hz is adequate for walking avatars).
- With htmx already in place, Server-Sent Events (htmx's SSE extension) plus plain `fetch` POSTs for one's own movement is a viable alternative to WebSockets for low-headcount rooms: SSE is one-directional but presence updates are small and infrequent; the cost is one open HTTP connection per visitor and slightly higher latency. A WebSocket is preferable above ~10 concurrent visitors or if proximity text chat is added.
- Text "proximity chat" (not voice) is cheap: reuse the same channel and SemPKM's existing comment model, scoped to visitors within N tiles. Voice should be deferred or delegated to an external LiveKit/Jitsi if ever wanted; the 4-user P2P mesh limit and the 15% TURN-failure figure show how quickly media becomes an ops burden.
- Asynchronous visiting (publish a room snapshot + store visitor traces as RDF in a shared named graph, synced by the existing RDF Patch/LDN machinery) requires zero real-time infrastructure, works across federated instances with no shared server, and is the model people use to "show off" a space. It is the recommended first step; live presence can be layered on later for same-instance members.
- Federation caveat (inference): live presence across *instances* would require one instance to host the room's WebSocket and the other's users to connect to it — i.e. a cross-origin WebSocket with WebID-based auth — which is more design work than same-instance presence. Asynchronous traces avoid this entirely because they travel as graph data.

### Gaps
- No official description of WorkAdventure's pusher/back zone protocol or of Gather's sync/SFU architecture was found; the gRPC/protobuf detail comes from a repo mirror.
- No benchmark found for bandwidth/CPU of presence-only 2D avatar sync at given user counts; the 20 Hz figure is Colyseus's default, not a measured requirement.
- I did not locate Colyseus's documented default `maxClients` in docs (source shows `Infinity`).
